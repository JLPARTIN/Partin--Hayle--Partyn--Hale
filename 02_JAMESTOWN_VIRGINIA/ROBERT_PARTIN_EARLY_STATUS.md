# Robert Partin — Early Virginia Status and Property Evidence

## What can presently be said
The muster itself does not support a characterization of Robert as an impoverished newcomer in 1625. He was household head with a house, arms/armor, food stores, livestock, wife, children and servants.

A scholarly biographical compilation by Martha W. McCartney describes him as an **ancient planter**, reports a February 1620 100-acre Henrico patent, mentions him in 1620 debtor/will material, and states that Virginia Company officials sent him substantial supplies in 1621. These details are valuable but their underlying citations must be checked before being promoted to primary-record status.

Nugent's *Cavaliers and Pioneers* independently indexes Robert among 1609 arrivals and points to later patent/General Court material.

## What this does NOT prove
It does not prove Robert was wealthy in England.
It does not prove he personally financed his 1609 passage.
It does not prove a Hayle patronage connection.
It does not establish his English birthplace.

## Research significance
The evidence does undermine simplistic biographies that treat “indentured servant from Bristol/Somerset” as an established fact without citation. Robert's actual legal/economic trajectory must be reconstructed from patents, Virginia Company records, General Court material and any surviving English records.
