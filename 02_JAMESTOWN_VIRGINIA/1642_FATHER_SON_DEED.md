# 13 April 1642 — Robert Partin the Elder and Robert Partin the Younger

## Major new bridge evidence
A compiled Virginia patent digest reproduced in later Partin research describes a Daniel Llewellyn/Lluellin patent containing **100 acres purchased from Robert Partin the elder**, land originally granted to Partin on **20 February 1619**, and states that **Robert Partin the elder and Robert Partin the younger, his son**, conveyed it to Llewellyn by deed dated **13 April 1642**.

This is more genealogically explicit than the 1655 Senior/Junior reference because the digest reportedly supplies the relationship: **“his son.”**

## Consequence
If the abstract is faithful to the patent, the father-son relationship between the immigrant Robert and the younger Robert is no longer merely inferred from the 1624/25 muster. The 1642 deed chain would explicitly identify the younger Robert as the elder Robert's son.

## Chronological fit
The younger Robert was four months old in January 1624/25. In April 1642 he would have been about 17½. That is young for an independent conveyance, so the exact deed/patent wording, legal capacity and dating need inspection. The age tension is a reason to inspect the original—not a reason to discard the record.
