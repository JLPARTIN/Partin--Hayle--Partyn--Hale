# Jamestown Virginia — Robert Partin, 1609–1657

## Executive Summary

Robert Partin arrived in Virginia in June 1609. He is recorded as a household head in the January 1624/25 muster, with wife Margaret, three young children, and two servants — one of whom, Thomas Hale/Hayle, was hanged in 1627 for a sexual offense against Robert and Margaret's daughter Avis. Land records place Robert (and later a Robert the "elder" alongside a Robert the "younger," his reported son) in Elizabeth City County and Shirley Hundred through the 1650s. This dossier consolidates what was previously ten-plus separate incremental research-lead files into a single narrative, and states plainly where the record chain is solid and where it still depends on derivative transcriptions this project has not personally verified against original patent-book or court images.

## Identity and Name Variants

Robert Partin / Partine / Parten. Household members: Margaret (wife); Robert Jr. (son); Avis, Rebecca, and (per one later headright list) Debora (daughters); Thomas Hale/Hayle (servant); Ellin Cooke (servant).

## Geographic Footprint

James City/Charles City Corporation (West & Shirley Hundred); Henrico corporation (1619/20 grant); Elizabeth City County (1637 lease, 1657 tenure); Isle of Wight County (1648 Seward patent, on a branch called Blackwater); Charles City County (1655 patent).

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented (1624/25 muster):** Robert Partin, wife Margaret, son Robert Jr. (infant), daughters Avis and Rebecca; servants Thomas Hale and Ellin Cooke.

**Added by the 1648 headright list, not independently corroborated elsewhere:** a fourth child, daughter Debora, not yet born at the 1624/25 muster.

**Strong documentary language (1642 deed, if the derivative abstract is faithful to the original):** Robert Partin "the younger" is explicitly called "his son" — i.e., son of Robert Partin "the elder." This is the strongest kinship-language record recovered anywhere in this project's Virginia-generation material.

**Chronological caution on the 1642 deed:** the infant Robert Jr. of the 1624/25 muster would have been approximately 17½ years old in April 1642 — young for an independent conveyance. This tension is a reason to obtain and inspect the original deed/patent wording (does the younger Robert sign or merely get named? what is his exact legal role?), not a reason to discard the record. It remains possible that "Robert the younger" in 1642 is not literally the 1624/25 infant but is nonetheless a documented son of Robert the elder from a different birth.

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence

The 1624/25 muster and the June 1627 General Court proceedings (both the offense record and the concealment record) are this dossier's strongest anchors; both are STRONG rather than PROVEN only because this project has not yet personally inspected an original manuscript or authoritative printed edition, not because their content is doubted. Every land record from 1637 onward is a derivative published abstract (chiefly Nugent's *Cavaliers and Pioneers* and secondary compilations reproducing patent-book language) — none has been checked by this project against a patent-book image.

## The 1627 General Court Proceedings — Full Detail

Two proceedings, evidently connected, were recovered:

**The offense record** reads in relevant part: "This Court take into consideracon the full and p[er]ticular informacon of God given unto it p[er]ticularly of the offense and abominable and damnable fact of Thomas Hayle committed upon Avis Partin the daughter of Robert Partin and Margarett his wife, and upon three other maiden children, and finding him guilty of the rape & Ravishment of the said foure maiden children, doe therefore order & adiudge that the said Thomas Hayle be hanged by the neck untill he be dead, and that Charles Maxey who was convicted of the like offense upon a young girl... shall be the executioner of the said Thomas Hayle. And that after the execution of the said Thomas Hayle, the said Charles Maxey shall be whipped at James Citie and after at Shirley Hundred. The Governor and Council doe order that the goods and estate of the said Thomas Hayle shall be forfeited, one third to the use of Avis Partin and the other two thirds to the use of the Governor & Council."

**The concealment record**, independently checked against a published transcription in *The Virginia Magazine of History and Biography* ("Minutes of the Council and General Court"), states that Margaret Partin of Shirley Hundred was punished — whipped, forty stripes — for concealing Thomas Hayle's offense, described as "lately executed," and for resisting disclosure after learning of it.

**What these records establish:** a direct legal association between Robert and Margaret Partin's family and Thomas Hayle, beyond the shared 1624/25 household muster; the specific offense (rape of four "maiden children," one being Avis Partin) for which Thomas was executed; and that Margaret had knowledge of the offense significant enough to be separately prosecuted for concealing it.

**What these records do NOT establish:** any kinship between Thomas Hayle and the Partin family. Thomas appears in both records only as the convicted offender; no biological or marital relationship between Margaret and Thomas is stated. Any relationship theory (aunt/nephew or otherwise) rests entirely on separate English-side evidence — see `01_ENGLAND_THAMES/ENGLAND_PARTIN_HAYLE_DOSSIER.md`.

**Research consequence:** these two proceedings should be read as connected — the concealment charge presupposes exactly the kind of prior knowledge a family member sheltering a household servant's victim might have — but no recovered document explicitly states the two are the same case. That connection is this project's own reasonable inference from shared names, dates, and locations, and should be confirmed against the original court minutes or McIlwaine's *Minutes of the Council and General Court of Colonial Virginia*.

## Migration and Land Corridor

A 1648 patent places the Partin household's headright claim on a branch called "Blackwater" in Isle of Wight County; a later 1706 Surry County record (see `03_SURRY_SUSSEX_VIRGINIA/SURRY_SUSSEX_VIRGINIA_DOSSIER.md`) places a Robert Partin on the north side of "Blackwater Swamp." **This shared place-name is a hypothesis worth testing, not a proven geographic continuity** — "Blackwater" recurs as a water-name across multiple, non-identical Virginia locations, and the two references cannot be equated without mapping the actual patent bounds and waterways. If a corridor can be mapped connecting Shirley Hundred/Elizabeth City (1620s–1650s) through the Blackwater patent (1648) to the Surry Blackwater Swamp tract (1706), that would materially strengthen the case for a continuous family rather than a same-name coincidence.

## Competing Reconstructions — The 1655→1706 Bridge Problem

The evidence supports shifting the project's central "lost generation" problem forward in time. A Robert Sr./Robert Jr. pair is documented together in the 1655 Shirley Hundred land chain. A Robert Partin is later documented in Southwark Parish, Surry County, in 1706 (see the Surry/Sussex dossier). The key open question is: **who is the 1706 Robert relative to the 1655 Senior/Junior pair?** Three candidate models remain open, none proven:

- **Model A** — the 1706 Robert is the same man as the 1624/25 infant / 1655 Robert Jr. He would be about 81 in 1706 — possible, but late-life land acquisition at that age would need to fit later death-chronology records.
- **Model B** — the 1706 Robert is a son of the 1655 Robert Jr., an intervening generation consistent with normal chronology and with many uncited online pedigrees, but requiring an actual parentage record this project does not yet have.
- **Model C** — the Surry man derives from a different, collateral colonial Partin branch entirely.

**Warning preserved from prior project sessions:** birth years such as "Robert III born 1655" appearing in user-contributed online trees are not evidence and must not be used to manufacture Model B by assumption. This project previously searched specifically for the "1677 inventory" of Robert Jr. sometimes cited in derivative pedigrees, and for a commonly asserted 14 June 1687 death date; **neither has been substantiated by adequate primary sourcing in any search this project has conducted.** Neither date should be promoted until an actual probate or court record is found.

## Contradictions

- This dossier's own body text (24 Nov 1637 lease chronology) is internally consistent, but a separately recovered document's summary table gives Robert Jr.'s death as "d. before 1677" in one place and "ca. 1623–1687" in its own final summary elsewhere — an internal inconsistency in that source, not a resolved fact, preserved here as a caution against citing either date without checking.
- See `11_SOURCES/SOURCE_CONTRADICTIONS/MASTER_CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md` #21–22 for the Robert Partin Sr. birth-year and origin-place conflicts, which bear on identifying which English candidate (if any) this Virginia Robert actually was.

## Rejected Claims

- The characterization of Robert Partin as a poor, indentured Bristol/Somerset arrival: the 1624/25 muster itself does not support this — Robert was a household head with a house, arms and armor, food stores, livestock, wife, children, and two servants. It does not prove he was wealthy in England, that he personally financed his passage, or that a Hayle patronage connection existed — it simply undermines the specific "poor indentured servant" characterization as an established fact stated without citation. See `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/REJECTED_AND_QUARANTINED_CLAIMS.md` #7 for the related Bristol/Somerset origin-theory rejection.
- A "family was preparing for a later move into North Carolina" characterization of the 1740s Virginia record set, found in some recovered derivative narrative documents, goes beyond what any Jamestown-era or Surry-era record actually states and should not be repeated as fact.

## Evidence Assessment

- **PROVEN:** Robert Partin's presence in early Virginia; his household's association with Thomas Hale/Hayle by 1624/25.
- **STRONG:** The 1624/25 muster's full content; the 1627 offense and concealment proceedings; the 1642 "elder/younger, his son" deed language (pending original inspection); the 1655 Senior/Junior land-chain reference.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** That the 1655 Robert Jr. and the 1706 Surry Robert are the same man, or directly related (Models A/B above); that the "Blackwater" place-name recurrence reflects a real migration corridor rather than coincidence.
- **CANDIDATE:** Debora as a fourth daughter (headright-list-only); the 1657 Hooke patent's "Robert Partin" identity (Sr., Jr., or other).
- **REJECTED:** "Poor indentured servant" characterization as an established, citable fact; Bristol/Somerset origin.

## Open Questions

2. Locate and inspect the alleged 1677 inventory and/or 1687 death record for Robert Jr. — neither has been substantiated by this project.
3. Map the Seward 1648 Blackwater patent, the Shirley Hundred/Elizabeth City tracts, and the 1706 Surry Blackwater Swamp tract against actual period waterways to test the migration-corridor hypothesis.
4. Resolve which Robert (Sr., Jr., or another) held the 1657 Elizabeth City tenure with Gresham Covell.
5. Determine the fate of "Robert the younger" after 1655/57 and test whether he — his son — or a collateral relative is the 1706 Surry Robert.
