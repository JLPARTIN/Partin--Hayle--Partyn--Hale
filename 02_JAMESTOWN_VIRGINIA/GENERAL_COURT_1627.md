# General Court — Thomas Hayle and Margaret Partin, 1627

## 2026 recovery: the underlying offense record

Recovery of the project owner's Google Drive corpus (September 2026) turned up a second, more specific transcription of General Court material from June 1627, apparently the offense record underlying Margaret Partin's later concealment prosecution described below. As recovered, it reads in relevant part:

> "This Court take into consideracon the full and p[er]ticular informacon of God given unto it p[er]ticularly of the offense and abominable and damnable fact of Thomas Hayle committed upon Avis Partin the daughter of Robert Partin and Margarett his wife, and upon three other maiden children, and finding him guilty of the rape & Ravishment of the said foure maiden children, doe therefore order & adiudge that the said Thomas Hayle be hanged by the neck untill he be dead, and that Charles Maxey who was convicted of the like offense upon a young girl... shall be the executioner of the said Thomas Hayle. And that after the execution of the said Thomas Hayle, the said Charles Maxey shall be whipped at James Citie and after at Shirley Hundred. The Governor and Council doe order that the goods and estate of the said Thomas Hayle shall be forfeited, one third to the use of Avis Partin and the other two thirds to the use of the Governor & Council."

**Status: STRONG.** This reads as a genuine primary-source transcription (period spelling, abbreviations, and bracketed uncertain readings are preserved as recovered) rather than a modern paraphrase, recovered from an AI-assisted research document rather than directly from an archive or scholarly edition by this project. **It has not yet been independently checked by this project against the original Virginia General Court manuscript or an authoritative printed edition** (e.g., H.R. McIlwaine's *Minutes of the Council and General Court of Colonial Virginia*), and should be treated as STRONG rather than PROVEN until that check is made.

**What this record establishes:** Thomas Hayle was convicted of a sexual offense against four children, one of them **Avis Partin, identified as "the daughter of Robert Partin and Margarett his wife."** This directly names Robert and Margaret Partin's daughter and gives the crime for which Thomas Hayle was executed — filling in what the previously-checked concealment record (below) left unspecified. Co-defendant Charles Maxey, separately convicted of a similar offense, was ordered to serve as Hayle's executioner before being whipped himself. One third of Hayle's forfeited estate was ordered paid to Avis Partin.

**What this record does NOT establish:** No kinship between Thomas Hayle and the Partin family. Thomas Hayle appears here only as the convicted offender; Avis Partin appears only as a named victim. Any relationship theory between Thomas Hayle and Margaret Partin — aunt/nephew or otherwise — rests entirely on separate evidence (see `01_ENGLAND_THAMES/LONDON_NETWORK.md` and `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md`, Claims 3 and 5), not on this record.

**Research consequence:** This record should be read as *explaining* the concealment charge against Margaret Partin below, not as a separate incident. A daughter's victimization by a household member she had "lately" known of would be exactly the kind of knowledge the concealment charge presupposes. The recovered corpus does not, however, contain a document that explicitly states the two proceedings are the same case — that connection is this project's own reasonable inference from the shared names, dates, and locations, and should be confirmed against the original court minutes.

## Checked published transcription
A digitized volume of *The Virginia Magazine of History and Biography* preserves a transcription from the Minutes of the Council and General Court. The text states that Margaret Partin of Shirley Hundred was punished for concealing the offense of Thomas Hayle, described as “lately executed,” and for resisting disclosure after learning of it.

The surviving published wording is important because it proves a direct legal association between **Margaret Partin of Shirley Hundred** and **Thomas Hayle** beyond the 1624/25 household muster.

## What the checked passage proves
- Margaret Partin was identified with Shirley Hundred.
- She knew of an offense committed by Thomas Hayle.
- She was accused of concealing it and resisting disclosure.
- Thomas had already been executed by the time of this court entry.
- The court ordered Margaret whipped with forty stripes.

## What this passage does not itself prove
The excerpt currently checked does **not** by itself establish Thomas's parentage, Margaret's maiden name, or a biological relationship between Margaret and Thomas. Those propositions require the other court material and/or English evidence.

## Research consequence
The Margaret–Thomas relationship is not based merely on two names occurring in the same 1625 household. The later court record demonstrates continuing interaction of sufficient intimacy or knowledge to involve Margaret directly in the criminal proceeding.
