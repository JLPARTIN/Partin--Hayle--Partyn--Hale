# Alabama — Marengo/Sumter County, a Collateral Branch (c.1790–1851)

## Executive Summary

This dossier is explicitly **collateral, not the project's direct line**. Its subject, **Asa B. Partin** (also given as "Parten"), is proposed — but not proven — to be a son of **Brasel Partin** (Barnabas Partin's brother; see `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`), which would make him Barnabas's nephew. The Jim Partin book's own author states the relationship plainly must still be established: "It is believed Asa was a son of Brasel Partin. This must be proven." Asa's own life is nonetheless independently well documented — Sumter District, SC residence, War of 1812 service, a documented dispute with Mt. Piscah Church, and a relocation to Marengo County, Alabama by 1836–1840, where he died in 1848. This dossier exists to keep his well-documented Alabama life available to the project without implying, by its placement in the directory structure, that his connection to the main Halifax-to-Mississippi line is settled.

## Geographic Footprint

Sumter District, South Carolina (residence through the 1830s, in the immediate vicinity of Burrell/Burril Cato's land); Marengo County, Alabama, specifically Nanafalia (residence from 1836–1840 until his 1848 death).

## Family Relationships

**Hypothesized, not proven:**
- Father: Brasel Partin — "believed," per the Jim Partin book's own explicit caveat, "must be proven." The evidence is entirely circumstantial: (1) chronological feasibility (Brasel old enough by the 1790s to be Asa's father), (2) geographic association (both documented in Halifax and again in the same 1816 Kershaw ledger), (3) a matching NC→SC→AL migration path, and (4) the elimination of David as an alternative candidate son of John Partin on age/migration-profile grounds by one recovered analysis. **No direct primary evidence (a birth record, or a father's will naming Asa) has been located.**
- Son: Isaac, per one recovered source, heir to SE¼ Section 27, Township 13, Range 1 East, Marengo Co., under Asa's nuncupative will.
- The 1851 quit-claim deed's co-grantors (Asa J. Parten, Wm. J. Parten, H.W. Parten, Joseph Parten, John Parten) are presumably additional children or heirs, not individually identified beyond their names on this document.

## The Distinctive "Brasil/Brasel" Name as a Collateral-Line Marker

The unusual given name Brasil/Brasel recurs at least once more in this project's broader recovered corpus: a FamilySearch-sourced profile identifies a later **Asa Brasil Partin**, father of Henry Washington Partin (b.1818, Sumter, SC) — a name overlap with this dossier's own Asa B. Partin that raises the possibility of a shared naming tradition within the extended family, but this has not been tested against any primary record and should not be assumed to represent the same lineage without independent proof. Any future research into this distinctive name should build every occurrence of Brasil/Brasel/Brazil Partin across NC, SC, and MS as a discrete collateral-identification strategy, since an unusually rare given name is potentially more discriminating evidence of kinship than the Partin surname alone — while never treating the rarity of a name as proof of kinship by itself.
