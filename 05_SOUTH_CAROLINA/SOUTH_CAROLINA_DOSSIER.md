# South Carolina — Kershaw/Camden District and Sumter District, c.1793–1813

## Executive Summary

The focal line's South Carolina generation centers on **Barnabas "Barney" Partin**, son of John Partin of Halifax County, NC (see `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/NORTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`), who relocated to the Claremont/Kershaw/Sumter District area around 1797, married Margaret "Peggy" Hux, held and sold land there in the immediate neighborhood of the Hux family through 1811, and then relocated on to Wilkinson County, Mississippi (see `06_MISSISSIPPI/MISSISSIPPI_DOSSIER.md`, which continues his biography). This dossier also covers his brother **Brasel Partin**, who followed a broadly similar path into the same South Carolina district but whose own downstream line is less documented, and who is the probable (not proven) father of **Asa B. Partin**, covered as a collateral branch in `07_ALABAMA/ALABAMA_DOSSIER.md`. A parallel, unresolved thread — "John Partin" (wife Winifred), possibly a brother of Barnabas — also surfaces in the Kershaw District by 1802; see the North Carolina dossier for the full "Three Johns" discussion.

This dossier is substantially strengthened by the September 2026 identification of the canonical source underlying much of this project's Barnabas material: *Barnabas Partin: Ancestors and Descendants, From Virginia to Mississippi and Beyond*, a book-length compiled genealogy by **Jim (Jimmy Dail) Partin** — a cousin of the project owner and, per internal evidence, the same "Jim Partin" who submitted the 1997 NCGenWeb query discussed in the North Carolina dossier.

## Identity and Name Variants

Barnabas / Barnaby / "Barney" / "Barnabus" Partin (the last spelling appears in at least one deed-index entry, preserved as an original variant); Brasel / Brasil / Brazil Partin.

## Geographic Footprint

Claremont County/Kershaw County (Camden District), South Carolina — specifically the neighborhood along Boggy Branch and Pine Tree Creek, in the immediate vicinity of Hux family landholding originating from an 1786 grant to William Hux; Sumter District.

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented:**
- Barnabas: father John Partin (d. 1795, Halifax Co.); wife Margaret "Peggy" [Hux?]; siblings per the same will — Robert "Robin" (executor), Brasel, David, Charles, Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, Elizabeth.
- Brasel: same father and sibling set; spouse unnamed in any recovered record.

**Working hypothesis, not proven:**
- Margaret's maiden name as Hux — see `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claim 14. The strongest single piece of evidence (the 6 Mar 1811 dower-relinquishment deed) is narrated but uncited, and the modern claim itself must be tested for circularity: a source counts as independent evidence only if it derives the Barnabas–Hux marriage from an actual record (a marriage bond/license, a deed naming both spouses, a Hux probate naming a daughter Margaret Partin, or a Partin probate naming wife Margaret) rather than from another online tree, an Ancestry public-tree profile, a copied FamilySearch relationship, or a Find a Grave biography — all of which could simply be repeating the same undocumented tradition. This project has not yet completed that independence test.
- Asa B. Partin as a son of Brasel — the Jim Partin book itself states plainly "It is believed Asa was a son of Brasel Partin. This must be proven." See `07_ALABAMA/ALABAMA_DOSSIER.md`.
- William Hux, found residing in Barnabas and Margaret's household in the 1850 Mississippi census (age 63), is speculated by the Jim Partin book to be "possibly Margaret's brother" — explicitly labeled speculation.

## The Hux Three-Generation Pattern

The Hux surname recurs with this family across three widely separated points: **1737**, Surry Co. VA — William Partin and Elizabeth Hux as co-witnesses to a will; **1767**, Halifax Co. NC — William and Ann Hux convey land to John Partin; and **this generation**, South Carolina — Barnabas Partin's wife repeatedly identified as Margaret/Peggy Hux, and William Hux's own Kershaw land (from an 1786 grant) directly bordering Barnabas's own land. Three competing explanations remain open: (A) one extended Hux family network remained associated with the Partins across multiple migrations and three generations; (B) the Hux events involve unrelated households that merely share a common surname; (C) one or more derivative pedigrees have contaminated later claims from the earlier, better-documented Hux association. **This project has not independently reconstructed the Hux family tree at any of the three points**, and doing so — without using the Partin tree to prove Hux relationships and then using those inferred Hux relationships to prove the Partin tree — is the necessary next step before this pattern can be treated as more than a suggestive coincidence. If independently verified, it would become one of the strongest non-surname migration signals in the entire project.

## FAN Network

**William Hux and Benjamin Hux** — the central FAN thread of this dossier, recurring as land neighbors and probable in-laws. **Samuel Payne, Robert "Robin" White, and Thomas English** — recurring Kershaw grantors/grantees in Barnabas's immediate transaction circle. **"Samuel Paym"** — possibly, but not confirmed, the same person as Samuel Payne; flagged as an open research priority. ## Contradictions

- Barnabas's SC-to-Thomas-English conveyance: dated 1811 (citing Kershaw SC DB F:240) in the more specific source, versus an earlier working note in this project's own history that had used 1807 — resolved in favor of 1811/DB F:240 as the better-cited figure, not a live open contradiction.
- The three-way birth/death date discrepancies for Barnabas himself (29 Aug 1773 vs. obituary-implied ~1772 vs. ~1769; 24 Mar 1855 vs. "the 25th") belong properly to his Mississippi years and are documented in full in `06_MISSISSIPPI/MISSISSIPPI_DOSSIER.md`.

## Rejected Claims

None specific to this South Carolina generation have been formally tested and rejected in the recovered corpus — the Hux marriage claim remains an untested working hypothesis (see above), not yet either confirmed or rejected.

## Evidence Assessment

- **STRONG:** Barnabas's Kershaw Co. residence and land transactions, 1800–1811 (census, deposition, neighboring-deed corroboration); Brasel's Halifax tax presence before relocation and his ca.1816 Kershaw ledger appearance.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Margaret's Hux origin; Asa B. Partin as Brasel's son; the broader three-generation Hux migration pattern; the exact timing and reasoning behind the 1810–1813 Mississippi relocation.
- **CANDIDATE:** William Hux (1850 household) as Margaret's brother; "Samuel Paym" as Samuel Payne.

## Open Questions

1. Locate the deed book and page for the 6 March 1811 Kershaw District, SC sale to Thomas English — the single strongest lead for proving Margaret's Hux parentage.
2. Independently reconstruct the Hux family tree in both Virginia/Halifax and Kershaw/Camden District, SC, without relying on Partin-tree-derived assumptions, to test the three-generation Hux hypothesis properly.
3. Confirm or disprove Asa B. Partin's parentage from Brasel Partin.
4. Recover Brasel Partin's own death/probate record in Kershaw County, currently dated only "ca. 1835" with no citation.
5. Determine whether "Samuel Paym" (the 20 Jan 1801 deposition) and "Samuel Payne" (Barnabas's later land grantor) are the same person.
