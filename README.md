# Partin–Hayle / Partyn–Hale Forensic Genealogy

## Thames to the James — and Beyond

This repository is the central research archive for the Partin / Parton / Partyn and Hayle / Hale families: their English origins, the early Virginia/Jamestown generation, the Surry–Sussex–Halifax descendants, later southern migrations, and Y-DNA evidence.

The project is intentionally **evidence-first**. It preserves proven facts, candidate identifications, rejected claims, unresolved bridges, and research targets separately rather than turning plausible reconstructions into pedigree facts.

## Research rules

1. **Primary record before pedigree.**
2. Preserve the spelling used by each source: Partin / Parton / Partyn / Partain; Hayle / Hale / Hales, etc.
3. Distinguish:
   - **PROVEN** — direct or converging documentary evidence establishes the proposition.
   - **STRONG** — substantial evidence, but a decisive link is still missing.
   - **WORKING HYPOTHESIS** — plausible and testable.
   - **CANDIDATE** — record/person worth investigating; identity not established.
   - **REJECTED / UNSUPPORTED** — evidence contradicts it or no adequate evidence supports it.
4. DNA is corroborative evidence, not a substitute for documentary parentage.
5. Every dossier should distinguish transcription, interpretation, and inference.
6. Negative searches matter and should be logged.
7. Never silently merge same-name people.

## Current high-value questions

The central English question is whether the London records identify the immigrant Robert Partin/Partyn and explain his relationship to the Hayle family. The central Virginia question is how the early Jamestown-era family connects, if at all, to the later documented Surry/Sussex Partin population. The central downstream question is the exact documentary path from Virginia into Halifax County, North Carolina and the later Carolinas–Mississippi line.

This repository is a living forensic case file. Claims will be upgraded or downgraded as original records are recovered and compared.
