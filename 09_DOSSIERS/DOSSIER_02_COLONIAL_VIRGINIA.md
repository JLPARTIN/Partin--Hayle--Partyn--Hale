# DOSSIER 02 — COLONIAL VIRGINIA CONTINUITY
## Jamestown-era Partins to the Surry/Sussex cluster

### Problem statement
The project must determine whether the early Virginia Partin household and the later Surry/Sussex Partins form one continuous family and, if so, reconstruct the exact generations.

Earlier work contains candidate/documentary anchors in 1655 and 1677. These are important because they mean the seventeenth century should not be described as a completely empty interval. Each record, however, must be reattached to its original citation and identity argument.

### Later Surry anchors
Compiled work records:
- 9 May 1706 — Robert Partin in Surry.
- 1721–22 — Tucker estate material involving Robert and Charles.
- Mar 1727 — Robert sells 200 acres to John Justice.
- Jun 1727 — Elizabeth acknowledges dower.
- ca. 1746 — Mary appears in Robert's later record environment.
- Aug 1746 — Robert dies; Mary administratrix; John receives 592 lbs tobacco.
- 1753–57 — Henry Partin appears as legally adult.

### Elizabeth versus Mary
The appearance of Elizabeth in 1727 and Mary by 1746 requires an explicit explanation. Possibilities include successive wives, multiple Roberts, transcription/identity error, or another legal relationship. The underlying deeds and court orders control.

### Charles, John and Henry
These men must be reconstructed as individuals before parentage is assigned. Estate payments, witnesses, land adjacency, tithables, road orders, church material and probate associations may establish relationships that a surname list cannot.

### Competing John identities
Project work has preserved both a traditional early-1720s John and a “John Charles” baptism candidate dated 16 Mar 1733. These cannot be silently merged. Each candidate needs a timeline and a compatibility test against later Halifax records.

### Method
Build a person-event table for every Partin variant in Surry/Sussex and adjacent jurisdictions; extract every associate; plot land; then test which men disappear as Halifax records begin.

### Current conclusion
Colonial continuity is plausible and increasingly documentable, but the repository will not publish a single uninterrupted father-son pedigree until the intermediate identities survive record-level testing.
