# DOSSIER 01 — FROM THE THAMES TO THE JAMES
## Robert Partin / Partyn and the Hayle / Hale Network, c. 1560–1627

### Executive assessment
The strongest current model does not begin with an assumed Bristol/Somerset indentured-servant origin. It begins with a London/Thames network whose chronology aligns unusually well with the people later documented together in Virginia.

The critical logic is:
1. Virginia places Thomas Hale/Hayle with Robert Partin.
2. Virginia evidence reportedly identifies Thomas as a son of Symon Hayle of London.
3. London research identifies a chronologically matching Thomas, son of Symon, in the St Mary Somerset network.
4. The same reconstructed family includes Margaret Hayle, a candidate for Margaret Partin.
5. London research also identifies a Robert Partyn candidate whose baptism date matches the Virginia age calculation.

No single resemblance proves the reconstruction. The value lies in independent pieces converging around the same network.

### The Robert problem
The Virginia Robert is a historical anchor; the London Robert is an identity candidate. The working test is whether the 1589 Robert can be connected to the Partyn household and then eliminated from English records after the expected migration period while appearing in Virginia.

A candidate John Partin/Partyn–Alice Gaskyn marriage (1586) and an earlier John Parton, son of Peter, at St Botolph Aldgate (1568) belong in the candidate tree, not the proven pedigree.

### The Hayle problem
Thomas is the bridge person. If the Virginia court record truly gives his father as Symon Hayle of London, it provides an English search target independent of later genealogy. A Thomas son of Symon baptized in 1605 is therefore far more meaningful than a random same-name hit.

The reconstructed Hayle family also contains Margaret, approximately Robert's generation. The proposed sibling relationship Symon–Margaret would make Thomas Margaret's nephew.

### Margaret's identity
The proposition **Margaret Hayle = Margaret Partin** currently explains the Virginia household elegantly but remains unproven. Required evidence includes a marriage, probate naming her married surname, or a sufficiently dense indirect proof eliminating alternatives.

### The “Somerset” error
St Mary Somerset was a London parish. Treating the parish name as proof of origin in Somerset county creates a geographic error. The inherited Bristol/Somerset narrative must therefore be rebuilt from actual evidence rather than repeated.

### Social-network hypothesis
The project is testing whether Hayle connections helped create Robert's Virginia opportunity. This is plausible, not established. Search predictions include occupational, shipping, parish, probate, patronage, apprenticeship, merchant, or Virginia Company connections.

### Occupation problem
The wording surrounding Symon Hayle's occupation—particularly “porter” and any apparently conflicting English description—must be transcribed directly. Early-modern occupational terms can be context-sensitive, but discrepancies cannot be harmonized without evidence.

### Evidence matrix
| Proposition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Robert Partin was present in early Virginia | PROVEN |
| Robert and Thomas appear together in Virginia household evidence | PROVEN/primary anchor |
| Thomas was son of a Symon Hayle of London | STRONG, pending attached exact court transcription |
| London Thomas (1605) = Virginia Thomas | STRONG CANDIDATE |
| London Robert Partyn (1589) = Virginia Robert | CANDIDATE |
| Symon and Margaret belong to same reconstructed Hayle family | STRONG WORKING RECONSTRUCTION |
| Margaret Hayle = Margaret Partin | WORKING HYPOTHESIS |
| Thomas was Margaret Partin's nephew | DEPENDENT HYPOTHESIS |
| Robert originated in Bristol/Somerset | UNSUPPORTED at present |
| Hayle network enabled migration | WORKING HYPOTHESIS |

### Falsification tests
The model weakens substantially if the London Robert is found living/dying in England after 1609; if the 1605 Thomas remains demonstrably in England after 1623; if Symon's family reconstruction excludes Margaret; or if the Virginia court father cannot be reconciled with the London Symon.
