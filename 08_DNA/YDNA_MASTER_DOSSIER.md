# Y-DNA Master Dossier

## Rule

Y-DNA can establish or strongly support patrilineal relatedness and relative branch order among living testers. It cannot, by itself, name the historical man in whom a given mutation arose, and it cannot substitute for documentary proof of any specific parent-child link in this project's paper genealogy. Every claim in this dossier that pairs a SNP/kit with a named historical ancestor is labeled a genealogical claim, not a genetic fact, per this rule. Participant-entered "earliest known ancestor" fields are documentary claims made by that participant, carrying exactly the evidentiary weight of that participant's own paper-trail research — which this project has not independently reviewed for any tester besides its own line.

## Executive Summary

This project has assembled Y-DNA evidence from three sources, recovered and consolidated September 2026: (1) a raw 19-row Y-STR/haplogroup spreadsheet, apparently used to visually cluster FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project participants by geography; (2) a narrative claim, recorded in the project owner's own draft Jamestowne Society application, that the owner's kit (MK85913) is an exact 700-marker match to other testers claiming descent from "Robert Partin b. 1655 England," under terminal SNP R-FTF47086; and (3) a series of prior project research notes tracking specific public FamilyTreeDNA project kit/SNP pairings over time. **The project's most important standing finding is a gap, not a match: SNP R-FTF47086 and kit MK85913 do not appear anywhere in the project's own raw 19-row spreadsheet**, meaning this project's two main DNA data sources cannot currently be cross-referenced against each other.

## The MK85913 / R-FTF47086 Claim

A separate, narrative (non-spreadsheet) document — a draft Jamestowne Society supplemental-application argument authored by the project owner — reports:

- Kit **MK85913**, tested to the Big Y-700 level, terminal SNP **R-FTF47086**.
- An exact match (0 genetic distance across all 700 markers tested) to other FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project participants who have entered "Robert Partin, b. 1655, England" as their own earliest known paternal-line ancestor.
- Supporting autosomal (AncestryDNA) evidence for the same kit: 25–80 cM matches to descendants of John Partin (d. 1795) via his sons Charles and Priscilla's lines; 15–45 cM matches to the Surry/Sussex Virginia Partin descendant cluster; 10–35 cM matches to Hux-surnamed testers from the Kershaw, SC, 1780s–1800s cluster, offered as support for Margaret "Peggy" Hux's identity (see `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`).

**What this establishes:** kits sharing a 0-genetic-distance 700-marker Y-DNA match share a genuinely recent common paternal ancestor — a real, meaningful genetic finding.

**What this does NOT establish:** that the shared ancestor is specifically "Robert Partin, b. 1655, England," or any other specific named person. A cluster of testers all naming the same documentary ancestor increases confidence that they researched consistently, not that the identification is correct; a shared, uncritically repeated tree error would produce the identical pattern.

**The cross-reference gap:** R-FTF47086 and kit MK85913 do not appear among the 19 rows of the raw spreadsheet above. Until this is resolved by re-pulling MK85913's placement directly from FamilyTreeDNA's current Partin Surname Project tree, this project cannot state how MK85913 relates to the documented Appalachian cluster, or whether R-FTF47086 is a downstream refinement of one of those branches or a separate, more distantly related line entirely.

## The Halifax/Sussex Branch Question

Prior project research reports observing a restructuring of the broader Parton/Partin/Partain FTDNA cluster beneath nodes FTT183/FTT184/FTT185, with documentary Halifax and Sussex lines reportedly remaining genetically separate from a newly merged branch. **This project has not verified this restructuring independently in the September 2026 recovery** and it should be re-confirmed against the live FTDNA tree before being relied upon. The underlying hypothesis, if confirmed, would be: if several documented branches merge beneath a downstream SNP while the Halifax and Sussex documentary lines remain outside it, this could indicate that Halifax and Sussex represent earlier-diverging paternal branches — a pattern potentially useful for locating a documentary/genetic split near the older Virginia generations. This remains a hypothesis, not a finding. Two predictions follow from it and should guide future testing: if Halifax and Sussex are genuinely early colonial splits, they should show distinct downstream terminal SNPs with a shared upstream Partin node, and their divergence should be genetically older than the mutations uniting any later-merged Group 1 lines; if instead one descends from the other in a later period, their branching topology should be nested rather than parallel. If a tester's DNA clusters with a different historical branch than their own documentary pedigree predicts, the conflict should be flagged and both evidence streams re-audited — a historical ancestor should never be moved merely to make the paper tree fit a SNP topology.

**Key test, not yet performed:** determine the immediate common upstream node of Halifax, Sussex, and any newly merged branches, then compare its age/formation interval with the documentary chronology. The documentary tree (built from deeds, wills, and censuses) and the genetic tree (built from SNP/STR results) should continue to be developed independently before being correlated, so that neither is used to silently validate the other.
