# London / Thames Network — Partyn and Hayle

## Research purpose
Reconstruct the English social network behind Robert Partin and Thomas Hayle rather than searching surnames in isolation.

## Partyn/Partin candidates
Current project work includes:
- John Parton baptized 4 Jul 1568 at St Botolph Aldgate, son of Peter — candidate only.
- John Partin/Partyn and Alice Gaskyn marriage candidate, 1586.
- Robert Partyn baptism candidate, 1 Jun 1589.

The 1589 date is important because the Virginia muster age for Robert points approximately to 1588/89. Chronological agreement is evidence of compatibility, not identity.

## Hayle candidates
The reconstructed cluster includes John Hayle, Symon Hayle, Margaret Hayle, Symon's wife Margaret Clement, and Thomas Hayle. Research centers on St Mary Somerset and associated London records.

Candidate chronology:
- Symon Hayle baptism: 3 Mar 1576.
- Margaret Hayle baptism: 25 Mar 1588.
- Symon Hayle–Margaret Clement marriage: 1601.
- Thomas Hayle baptism: 19 May 1605.
- Symon burial candidate: 28 Mar 1626.

## St Mary Somerset correction
“St Mary Somerset” is a London parish name. A record attached to that parish cannot be converted merely from the word *Somerset* into evidence that the person came from Somerset county or Bristol. Any Bristol/Somerset origin theory requires independent evidence.

**Owner-confirmed (Oct 2026):** a Bristol/Gloucestershire/Somerset origin theory for Robert Partin Sr., recovered from the September 2026 Drive corpus, has been directly disclaimed by the project owner as an unsourced working theory produced while sorting through conflicting online family trees — not a documented lead, and not to be revisited absent an actual new citation. See `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/REJECTED_AND_QUARANTINED_CLAIMS.md` #7. The St. Mary Somerset, London research on this page — the Hayle family and the associated Partyn/Partin candidates — is the project's retained, source-backed English-origins thread and should continue to be the focus of English-side research, even though its individual candidate identifications remain unproven and require independent archival verification.

## Network hypothesis
If Margaret Hayle became Margaret Partin, Thomas would be her nephew under the current sibling reconstruction. Thomas later appearing in Robert Partin's Virginia household would then have a direct kinship explanation.

That is a powerful hypothesis because it predicts additional evidence: probate kinship language, shared witnesses, parish overlap, apprenticeship/livery ties, occupational connections, or migration sponsorship. It remains a hypothesis until those predictions are tested.

## 2026 recovery: Symon Hayle's will contradicts the Margaret-Hayle premise

Recovery of the project owner's Google Drive corpus (September 2026) turned up a transcription of the will of **Symon Hayle, tailor of the parish of St. Mary Somerset, London**, dated 10 Feb 1625 (1625/26 by modern reckoning). As recovered, the will:

- Bequeaths £100 to son **Thomas Hayle**, payable at age 21, with a contingency splitting that sum £50/£50 between son **Robert Hayle** and daughter **Anne Hayle** if Thomas died before 21.
- Bequeaths £10 to "my brother Thomas Hayle" (an uncle of the same name).
- Names "my sister Anne Fisher," "my sister Joane [surname uncertain]," and "my cosin Symon [surname uncertain]."
- Names wife **Elizabeth Hayle** as residuary legatee and sole executrix.
- **Does not name a daughter Margaret.**

**This is significant negative evidence against Claim 3 in `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md`: that Margaret Partin's maiden name was Hayle, as a daughter of this Symon Hayle household.** The recovered analysis of this will states the point directly: a will naming surviving and contingent children, and not including a Margaret, weighs against Margaret Partin having been Symon's daughter — though it does not rule out Margaret belonging to a different, unreconstructed branch of the Hayle family, having died or married out before 1625, or the connection running through some other relationship entirely.

**Status change: the "Margaret Hayle → Margaret Partin" identification is downgraded from WORKING HYPOTHESIS to REJECTED pending new evidence.** The aunt/nephew reconstruction above (built on the premise that Margaret was herself a Hayle) currently has no documented mechanism to explain Thomas Hayle's presence in Robert and Margaret Partin's Virginia household — that presence remains **unexplained by any kinship theory currently supported by a primary record.** See `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/GENERAL_COURT_1627.md` for the recovered 1627 offense record naming Thomas Hayle's victim as Avis Partin, "the daughter of Robert Partin and Margarett his wife" — which establishes the Hayle/Partin legal entanglement but, again, no kinship.

**This will transcription has not been independently checked by this project against the original Guildhall/London Metropolitan Archives probate record.** Treat the child list and relationships above as STRONG (a specific, detailed, internally consistent transcription with period-appropriate names and bequest structure) rather than PROVEN.

**Research consequence:** the project's next step on the English side should be to (a) verify this will directly against LMA holdings, (b) search independently for Margaret's actual origin without assuming a Hayle connection, and (c) reconsider whether Thomas Hayle's household placement with the Partins reflects a servant/master relationship with no family tie at all, rather than continuing to search for a kinship explanation the evidence does not currently support.
