# England — The Partyn / Hayle Network, c.1560–1627

## Executive Summary

This dossier covers the earliest, least certain stage of the project's single documented line: the possibility that the Robert Partin who appears in Virginia from 1609 can be traced to a specific London family network, and that Thomas Hayle — who appears in Robert's Virginia household in 1624/25 and was hanged there in 1627 — can be identified as the son of a specific London tailor, Symon Hayle. No single English record proves any of this. The project's value here lies in a set of chronologically compatible candidate identifications that converge around one Thames-side London network rather than in any one proven fact. A September 2026 recovery of the project owner's research corpus added the single most important document in this dossier — Symon Hayle's own 1625/26 will — and it *weakens*, rather than strengthens, the project's oldest working hypothesis (that Margaret Partin was born a Hayle). This dossier states plainly, throughout, which propositions are PROVEN, STRONG, WORKING HYPOTHESIS, CANDIDATE, or REJECTED.

## The Core Logic

1. Virginia places Thomas Hale/Hayle in Robert Partin's household in 1624/25, and Thomas is executed there in 1627 (PROVEN — see `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA_DOSSIER.md`).
2. Virginia court material is reported (though not yet independently checked by this project against an original manuscript) to identify Thomas as connected to a Symon Hayle of London.
3. London parish research around St. Mary Somerset identifies a chronologically matching Thomas, son of a Symon Hayle, baptized 1605.
4. The same reconstructed Hayle family includes a Margaret Hayle — for decades this project's leading candidate for Margaret Partin. **Symon Hayle's own 1625/26 will, recovered in September 2026, does not name a daughter Margaret — significant negative evidence against this specific identification (see below).**
5. London research separately identifies a Robert Partyn baptismal candidate whose date is chronologically compatible with the Virginia age calculation.

No single resemblance proves the reconstruction. The historical value lies in independent pieces converging on the same network, and equally in being honest about which pieces have just been weakened or eliminated.

## Family Relationships

### The reconstructed Hayle family (per Symon Hayle's 1625/26 will, recovered September 2026)

Symon Hayle, tailor of St. Mary Somerset, London, wrote a will on 10 Feb 1625 (1625/26) that:
- Bequeaths £100 to son **Thomas Hayle**, payable at age 21, with a contingency splitting that sum £50/£50 between son **Robert Hayle** and daughter **Anne Hayle** if Thomas died before 21.
- Bequeaths £10 to "my brother Thomas Hayle" (an uncle of the same name).
- Names "my sister Anne Fisher," "my sister Joane [surname uncertain]," and "my cosin Symon [surname uncertain]."
- Names wife **Elizabeth Hayle** as residuary legatee and sole executrix.
- **Does not name a daughter Margaret.**

This will reads as a genuine, detailed primary-source transcription (period-appropriate names and bequest structure), but has not yet been independently checked by this project against the original Guildhall/London Metropolitan Archives probate record — treat its contents as STRONG, not PROVEN.

### Margaret Hayle = Margaret Partin — REJECTED (downgraded September 2026)

For years this project's leading working hypothesis was that Margaret Partin (Robert's wife in Virginia) was born Margaret Hayle, sister of Symon, making Thomas her nephew. **Symon Hayle's own will does not name a daughter Margaret**, which is meaningful negative evidence against this specific identification — a will naming surviving and contingent children, and omitting a Margaret, weighs against Margaret Partin having been Symon's daughter. It does not rule out Margaret belonging to a different, unreconstructed branch of the Hayle family, having died or married out before 1625, or a connection through some other relationship entirely.

**Consequence:** Thomas Hayle's presence in Robert and Margaret Partin's Virginia household in 1624/25 is currently **unexplained by any kinship theory supported by a primary record.** The aunt/nephew hypothesis (Thomas as Margaret's nephew via a Hayle-born Margaret) has no documented mechanism left to stand on. Two live possibilities remain open, neither proven: (a) Margaret belongs to an as-yet-unreconstructed branch of the Hayle family, or (b) Thomas's household placement reflects a servant/master relationship with no family tie to Margaret at all — Thomas is explicitly classified as a **servant** in the 1624/25 muster, and that fact must be retained regardless of any kinship theory later proven or disproven.

### Robert Partyn (English candidate) = Robert Partin (Virginia immigrant)

Working test: whether the 1589 baptismal candidate can be connected to a Partyn household and then shown to disappear from English records after the expected migration period while appearing in Virginia. Not tested to any conclusion by this project. A candidate John Partin/Partyn–Alice Gaskyn marriage (1586) and an earlier John Parton, son of Peter, at St Botolph Aldgate (1568), belong in the candidate tree only, not a proven pedigree.

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence

The only document in this dossier approaching primary-source quality is the recovered Symon Hayle will transcription. Every other English-side identification in this dossier rests on secondary genealogical research (baptismal/marriage index candidates) that this project has not itself verified against parish registers or the London Metropolitan Archives.

## The Social-Network / Migration-Sponsorship Hypothesis

The project has long tested whether Hayle family connections helped create Robert Partin's opportunity to migrate to Virginia. This remains plausible, not established. It predicts additional evidence this project has not yet found: probate kinship language, shared witnesses, parish overlap, apprenticeship/livery ties, occupational connections, or Virginia Company sponsorship records. An unresolved detail from prior project sessions — the exact wording surrounding Symon Hayle's occupation (a "porter" description has been reported alongside a "tailor" description in different sources; the recovered will above states "taylor") — should be reconciled directly against the original probate record rather than harmonized by assumption.

## Competing Reconstructions

## Evidence Assessment

- **PROVEN:** Nothing on the English side stands alone as proven; every English-side identification depends on a Virginia-side anchor (Thomas Hayle's presence and execution) that is itself only STRONG, not independently verified by this project against an original manuscript.
- **STRONG:** Symon Hayle's 1625/26 will as a document (pending LMA verification); the Thomas Hayle (b.1605) candidate as chronologically compatible with the Virginia Thomas.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Social-network/migration-sponsorship theory; the Robert Partyn (1589) candidate as the Virginia immigrant.
- **CANDIDATE:** John Parton (1568), John Partin/Alice Gaskyn marriage (1586), Margaret Hayle (1588) baptism as an unplaced Hayle-family member.
- **REJECTED:** Margaret Partin = Margaret Hayle (daughter of Symon); Bristol/Somerset origin for Robert Partin Sr.

## Falsification Tests

The whole English-network model weakens substantially if: the London Robert Partyn candidate is found living or dying in England after 1609; the 1605 Thomas Hayle candidate is found demonstrably still in England after 1623; Symon Hayle's reconstructed family, once fully verified, excludes any plausible Margaret connection at all; or the Virginia court material's "son of Symon Hayle" attribution cannot be reconciled with the actual London Symon Hayle upon direct manuscript review.

## Archival Program — What Would Resolve This

1. Direct verification of Symon Hayle's will against the original Guildhall/London Metropolitan Archives probate record.
2. Direct verification of the Virginia court record's wording connecting Thomas Hayle to "Symon Hayle of London" against an original manuscript or authoritative printed edition (see `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA_DOSSIER.md`).
3. Original images for the Robert Partyn (1589), John Partin/Alice Gaskyn (1586), and John Parton (1568) baptismal/marriage candidates.
4. A reconstructed Hayle sibling set complete enough to either place Margaret in it or definitively exclude her.
5. Apprenticeship, freemen/livery, subsidy/tax, and Virginia Company-associated record searches for both the Partyn and Hayle candidate families.
6. Negative searches: English records for either candidate family postdating their proposed migration/death dates, which would falsify the identification.
