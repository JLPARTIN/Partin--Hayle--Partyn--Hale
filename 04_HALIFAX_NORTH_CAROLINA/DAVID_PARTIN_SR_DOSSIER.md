# David Partin Sr. — Halifax County, North Carolina (c.1780–1835)

## Executive Summary

David Partin Sr. is documented as a Halifax County, North Carolina taxpayer and landowner in the early nineteenth century, most concretely through a 4 December 1823 deed conveying his interest in land inherited from his father and, separately, "from the death of my mother," on a tract called "Robert Partin's tract." His parentage (commonly asserted as a son of John Partin, d. 1795) rests on a single crowd-sourced online tree and is not established by any primary record recovered by this project. His wife's identity is the subject of five successive AI-assisted research drafts recovered from the project's Google Drive in September 2026 — and remains genuinely unresolved: the drafts do not converge toward an answer so much as cycle through one, discard it, and reassert it without engaging their own prior findings. This dossier exists specifically to document that evolution honestly, per this project's evidence standard, rather than presenting the most recent draft's confident language as settled fact.

## Geographic Footprint

Halifax County, North Carolina, specifically Tax Districts 1–3 and 5 as referenced across the recovered drafts; the Darling Swamp/Fishing Creek neighborhood associated with the broader John Partin (d. 1795) family home plantation.

## Family Relationships

- **Explicitly documented:** None. No primary record recovered by this project names David Partin Sr.'s parents, wife, or children directly.
- **Strongly inferred, weakly sourced:** Parentage from John Partin (d. 1795) and wife Mary — sourced in every recovered draft exclusively to a single WikiTree page (Partin-664), which itself supplies no primary citation for the claim.
- **Hypothesized, never resolved despite five attempts:** Wife's identity. See "Evolution of the Investigation" below.
- **Speculative, no supporting record:** A son Stephen Partin (b. c.1802) said to have moved to Georgia — appears only in the corpus's more speculative later drafts, with no citation.

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence

The 1823 deed is the only record in this dossier with genuine image-level citation specificity (DGS/image number, manuscript page range) supplied consistently across multiple independent drafts. Every other claim in this dossier — the 1797 tax entry aside — traces to secondary web compilations (WikiTree, NCGenWeb) or to the drafts' own prior, unverified drafts cited as if they were sources.

## Land and Property

The 1823 deed's subject matter is itself the clearest land evidence: David held (and sold) an interest in land his father had held, and a separate interest inherited through his mother, on a tract known as "Robert Partin's tract" — a name that also surfaces independently in a separately recovered 1823 Halifax deed involving a different Partin grantor (see `HALIFAX_RECORD_TARGETS.md` and the Robert "Robin" Partin material in `JOHN_PARTIN_1762_1795_DOSSIER.md`), suggesting a family land tract that passed through multiple heirs of the 1795 testator — a connection this project has not yet proven with a title chain.

## Probate and Estate

No probate record for David Partin Sr. himself was recovered.

## FAN Network

Thomas Revelle/Neville (1823 grantee — his surname is given both ways across the recovered drafts, an unresolved transcription discrepancy); the Hawkins family (Philemon, David, Nancy, Sarah Hawkins) and the Lane, Joyner, Alsobrook, and Brantley families appear as Halifax tax-district neighbors in the later, more narrative drafts, proposed there as circumstantial context for the wife-identity question rather than as proven kin.

## Competing Reconstructions / Evolution of the Investigation

Five successive AI-assisted drafts of the same question — "who was David Partin Sr.'s wife?" — were recovered. Read in sequence, they show the confidence of the answer rising even as the underlying evidence does not change:

1. **Draft 1 ("David Partin Sr." field worksheet).** Bare methodology and a list of records to pull. Offers no conclusion. Establishes the 1823 deed as the strongest lead and explicitly warns against leaping from a disputed word in that deed to any specific name: "any leap from Kurson? to Cousins/Keziah without corroboration" is flagged as invalid.
2. **Draft 2 ("Identifying The Wife of David Partin Sr.").** The most disciplined draft in the series. States plainly and repeatedly what the evidence does *not* support: Keziah as a proven given name, Revelle or Hawkins as a proven maiden family, or the 1823 deed as containing any actual wife's signature or dower release. Final verdict: **"IDENTITY UNRESOLVED."**
3. **Draft 3 ("David Partin Sr.'s Wife Identification").** A methodological regression. Introduces the WikiTree-sourced parentage claim (John Partin as father, birth year 1780), a "Deed Book 26" citation for the 1823 deed not found in the more careful drafts, and a District 2 neighbor survey (Hawkins, Lane, Joyner families). Despite acknowledging the wife's name is "not stated in a single, surviving marriage bond," its concluding synthesis asserts she "was a member of the Revelle or Hawkins family, and likely carried the name Keziah" — a direct reversal of Draft 2's explicit prohibition on exactly this claim.
4. **Draft 4 ("Identifying David Partin Sr.'s Wife").** Adds a formal paleographic transcription exercise on the disputed word in the 1823 deed's inheritance clause (which names David's *mother*, not his wife — a conflation Draft 2 had specifically warned against). This draft reads the word as "Kursin," proposes a "Kirstin" (Scottish form of Christina) hypothesis, and states it has "definitively" ruled out "Keziah" as the deed's actual word — but supplies no image, no archival citation, and no completed letterform comparison to support that conclusion; the comparison it proposes as the actual verification step is explicitly left undone.
5. **Draft 5 ("Forensic Genealogical Analysis of the Marital Identity of David Partin Sr....").** The most recent and most polished draft. Silently drops Draft 4's "Kursin" finding entirely — it is not mentioned, engaged, or refuted — and reverts to Draft 3's Keziah/Revelle/Hawkins framing, stated in the most declarative language of the whole series: "the wife of David Partin Sr. is identified as a daughter of the local kinship network in District 2, likely of the Revelle or Hawkins lineage."

**Current status: UNRESOLVED.** The only claim that survives all five drafts with a consistent, real citation is the 1823 deed's existence and content, which does not name a wife at all. No draft reports having located, opened, or quoted from: (a) a deed where David Partin Sr.'s wife is named as a joining grantor or dower-releaser, (b) a tax-list widow entry tied to David, (c) an administration or estate record naming his widow, or (d) the "Partin Family Bible" that every draft names as the record that would resolve this question. This dossier's job is to preserve that gap honestly, not to average the drafts into a confident-sounding answer none of them actually earned.

## Contradictions

- Grantee surname: "Thomas Revelle" (most drafts) vs. "Thomas Neville" (one independently recovered land-indenture analysis describing what appears to be the same 1823 transaction, consideration $50, same date). Not resolved.
- The disputed word in the 1823 deed's maternal-inheritance clause: unread in Draft 1–2, read as supporting "Keziah" in Draft 3, read as "Kursin"/"Kirstin" and explicitly *not* Keziah in Draft 4, and simply unaddressed in Draft 5.
- Deed book citation: "DGS 007547227, image 629 of 774" (Draft 1, most specific) vs. "Halifax Deed Book 26" (Drafts 3 and 5, uncited, self-referential to Draft 1 rather than to an index).

## Rejected Claims

None formally rejected yet — unlike the John Partin (d. 1795) wife-identity question (see `MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claim 10), no recovered document performed a dedicated negative-evidence search on the Keziah/Revelle/Hawkins/Kursin candidates for David's wife. This is itself a gap: the corrective document that exists for "Mary Hewitt" has no counterpart here.

## Evidence Assessment

- **STRONG:** The 1823 deed's core content (grantor, grantee, date, inheritance-through-father and inheritance-through-mother's-death language, "Robert Partin's tract" phrase).
- **CANDIDATE:** Halifax residency and 1797 insolvent tax status.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Parentage from John Partin (d. 1795); wife's identity in any form (Keziah, Revelle, Hawkins, or Kursin/Kirstin).

## Open Questions

1. What is the actual word in the 1823 deed's maternal-inheritance clause? This requires a direct, documented letterform comparison against the manuscript image — not another narrative assertion.
2. Is there a primary source (deed, tax list, marriage bond, or estate record) that actually names David Partin Sr.'s wife?
3. Does the "Partin Family Bible" referenced across multiple drafts as the presumptive answer actually exist and survive, and has anyone in this research program actually opened it?
4. What is David's relationship, if any, to Robert "Robin" Partin's 1821 Darling Swamp deed and the broader "Robert Partin's tract" land cluster discussed in `JOHN_PARTIN_1762_1795_DOSSIER.md`?
