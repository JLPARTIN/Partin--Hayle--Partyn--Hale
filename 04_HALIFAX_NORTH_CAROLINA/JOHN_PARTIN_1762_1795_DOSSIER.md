# John Partin — Halifax County, North Carolina (d. 1795)

## Executive Summary

A John Partin was taxed in Halifax County, North Carolina from at least 1762, purchased land there through the 1760s–1780s, and died in 1795, leaving a will that named a wife Mary and multiple children. He is the best-documented single individual in the Halifax generation of this project and the anchor point ("John A" in this project's identity-separation framework) from which the Barnabas Partin and David Partin Sr. lines both descend. His parentage — whether he is the same John Partin paid from a Surry County, Virginia, Robert Partin's estate in 1746 — is not established. His will's exact contents are reported at least three incompatible ways across the recovered research corpus and have not been confirmed against a primary image by this project. This dossier consolidates the September 2026 Google Drive recovery; see `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claims 8–11 for the underlying claim-by-claim evidence grading and `08_SOURCES/SOURCE_CONTRADICTIONS/MASTER_CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md` for the will-contents conflict in full.

## Identity and Name Variants

Partin / Parten / Parton / Partain — no variant spellings specific to this individual beyond the general family pattern are documented. He is distinguished in this project's working framework from two contemporaries of the identical name active in the same region and period:

- **"John A"** — this individual, wife Mary, d. 1795 Halifax.
- **"John B"** — wife Winifred, active Halifax 1793 → Kershaw District, SC 1802; relationship to John A unresolved (see below).
- **"John C"** — Orange County, NC collateral line, son of a different William Partin (will proved 1804); explicitly unconnected to John A or B by every document that discusses him.

A distinct, still-later Halifax-area "John Partin" documented via an 1884 Wake County deed (son Jefferson Davis Partin dividing "the lands of his late father John Partin dec'd") is a fourth, later-generation individual who must not be conflated with any of the above.

## Geographic Footprint

Surry and Sussex Counties, Virginia (presumed origin, unproven); Charles City Co. VA (1761 court appearance, if the same man); Halifax County, NC (primary residence from 1762); Edgecombe County, NC (adjoining jurisdiction, several 1770s records); Bute County, NC (predecessor jurisdiction to part of Halifax/Warren, road-crew record).

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented (per the version of the 1795 will corroborated by the largest number of independent research sessions and by the Jim Partin book):**
- Wife: Mary (surname not established — see Claim 10, "Hewitt" is REJECTED).
- Sons: Robert ("Robin," executor, home plantation), Brasel/Brasil, Barnabas ("Barney"), David, Charles.
- Daughters: Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, Elizabeth.
- Will witnesses William Partin and Jesse Partin are identified across multiple independent sources as a **cousin and nephew, not sons** — sons of a different, collateral William Cocke Partin/Jesse Partin line. This is a specific, multiply-corroborated correction of a common public-tree error and should not be reversed without new evidence.

**Strongly inferred, not proven:**
- Parentage from a Surry Co. VA Robert Partin (III or IV, depending on the source's numbering) and a wife identified in different sources as Elizabeth Rawlings or Martha — see Claim 8. No primary document in the recovered corpus states this filiation directly.

**Hypothesized, actively disputed:**
- "John B" (wife Winifred) as a son of John A — three different confidence levels given across the corpus, ranging from "Unknown" to asserted fact. See Claim 11.

**Rejected:**
- Wife's maiden name "Hewitt" — see Claim 10.
- A "1705 Surry will" sometimes used to identify this John Partin — flagged in the recovered corpus itself as a chronological impossibility if the ca. 1722/25 birth estimate is used (a testator active in 1705 cannot be the father of, or the same age as, a man not yet born).

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence

No document in the recovered research corpus reports personal image-review of the will, the 1767 Hux deed, the 1762 or 1782 tax lists, or the 1793 Winifred deed. Every citation traces to a secondary AI-assisted research session or to the 1997 NCGenWeb query. The single externally authored secondary source in the corpus — the Jim Partin book, *Barnabas Partin: Ancestors and Descendants* — independently reports the 1795 will naming sons Robert, Brasel, Barnabas, David, Charles, which is the version of the will this dossier currently treats as the working reading (see Contradictions below for the competing versions).

## Land and Property

1767 Hux purchase (100 acres, Great Darlling Swamp); 1772 Edgecombe sale to Elias Fort (100 acres, Swift Creek — note this may represent a different, Edgecombe-resident John Partin/Partain rather than this dossier's Halifax subject; not resolved in the recovered corpus); 1782 holding of 294 acres with 2 horses and 7 cattle, the most concrete data point recovered for his estate at maturity.

## Probate and Estate

Will dated 13 Jul 1795, proved August 1795 session, Halifax County Court — this date/session combination is the version supported by the largest number of independent sources. Competing citations exist for the will's exact page (Will Book 3, p. 248 vs. p. 251 vs. p. 256) and even its date (one narrative document gives "2 January 1795" instead). **This is the single highest-priority archival target for the entire Halifax generation of this project.**

## FAN Network

William Reeves (1762 tax-list associate, a Virginia migrant himself per several sources); William and Ann Hux (1767 land grantors — the Hux family recurs across three later generations of this project, see `07_FAN_NETWORKS/HUX_PARTIN_NETWORK.md`); Elias Fort (1772 grantee, himself a Sussex/Surry VA migrant per multiple sources — proposed as a possible "conduit" figure for the VA→NC migration corridor, though this is explicitly circumstantial in every source that raises it); Francis Floyd and son Benjamin Floyd (1770 apprenticeship).

## Competing Reconstructions

See Claim 8 (parentage) and Claim 11 (Three Johns) in `MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` for the full competing-confidence-level analysis. In short: an advocacy-framed Jamestowne Society application draft in the recovered corpus treats the entire Robert Sr.(1609)→...→John(1795) chain as settled; multiple neutral, self-auditing documents in the same corpus explicitly call the Surry-to-Halifax bridge "the biggest problem" in the whole research program. This dossier follows the more conservative reading.

## Contradictions

See `08_SOURCES/SOURCE_CONTRADICTIONS/MASTER_CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md`, entries on the 1795 will contents/page/date, the 1746 payment amount, and the Elizabeth (1761)/Mary (1795) wife-name discrepancy.

## Rejected Claims

- Wife's maiden name "Hewitt" (Claim 10).
- Any single-document treatment of "John Partin" 1674–1802 as one continuous man — the recovered corpus's own six-identity-packet analysis explicitly rejects this, distinguishing at least six discrete "John Partin" clusters active in Virginia and North Carolina in this period, of which only "John A" (this dossier) is this project's direct line.

## Evidence Assessment

- **STRONG:** Halifax residency from 1762; land acquisition and tax presence through the 1780s; death and probate in 1795.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Virginia origin and parentage from a Surry Co. Robert Partin; identity of "John B" as his son.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Exact will contents and citation; wife's maiden name.
- **REJECTED:** "Hewitt" maiden name; any 1705 Surry will as directly identifying this man.
