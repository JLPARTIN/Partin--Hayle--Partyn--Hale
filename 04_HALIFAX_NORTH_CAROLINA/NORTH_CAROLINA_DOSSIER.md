# Halifax County, North Carolina — The Focal Line (c.1760–1835)

**This is the focal point of the entire project.** The project owner's own documented paternal line runs through Barnabas Partin (see `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md` and `06_MISSISSIPPI/MISSISSIPPI_DOSSIER.md`), and Barnabas's own origin is here: a John Partin taxed in Halifax County, North Carolina from at least 1762, who died in 1795 leaving a will naming a wife Mary and multiple children. Every other geographic dossier in this project — England, Jamestown, Surry/Sussex on one side; South Carolina, Mississippi, Alabama, and the Appalachian collateral lines on the other — exists to explain how the family arrived at Halifax County and where it went afterward. This dossier is deliberately the most detailed in the project.

## Executive Summary

John Partin appears in Halifax County tax records from 1762, acquires land there through the 1760s–1780s, and dies in 1795, leaving a will naming wife Mary and (per the best-corroborated reading) sons Robert, Brasel, Barnabas, David, and Charles, and daughters Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, and Elizabeth. His parentage — whether he is the same John Partin paid from a Surry County, Virginia, Robert Partin's estate in 1746 — is not established; this is the project's single most important unresolved generational bridge. His will's exact contents and even its date/page citation are reported at least three incompatible ways across the recovered research corpus and have never been confirmed against a primary image by this project. A separate John Partin, wife Winifred, active in Halifax and later Kershaw District, SC, must be kept analytically distinct pending proof either way of a family relationship. This dossier consolidates twenty-six previously separate incremental research files, the project's own two most developed person dossiers (John Partin and David Partin Sr.), and the September 2026 Google Drive recovery into one narrative.

## Identity and Name Variants

Partin / Parten / Parton / Partain. Three (at minimum) contemporaneous men named John Partin must be kept separate in this project's working framework:

- **"John A"** — this dossier's central subject, wife Mary, d. 1795 Halifax.
- **"John B"** — wife Winifred, active Halifax 1793 → Kershaw District, SC 1802; relationship to John A unresolved.
- **"John C"** — Orange County, NC collateral line, son of a different William Partin (will proved 1804); explicitly unconnected to John A or B by every document that discusses him.

A fourth, later-generation Halifax-area "John Partin" is documented via an 1884 Wake County deed (son Jefferson Davis Partin dividing "the lands of his late father John Partin dec'd") and must not be conflated with any of the above.

## Geographic Footprint

Surry and Sussex Counties, Virginia (presumed origin, unproven); Charles City Co., VA (1761 court appearance, if the same man); Halifax County, NC (primary residence from 1762, specifically Tax Districts 1, 2, 3, and 5 across the family's various members); Edgecombe County, NC (adjoining jurisdiction, several 1770s records); Bute County, NC (predecessor jurisdiction to part of Halifax/Warren, road-crew record); the Darling Swamp/Fishing Creek neighborhood, identified across multiple sources as the family's home-plantation area.

The only genuine Partin/Hewitt connection found anywhere in this project's Halifax material is the 1777 Barilla Hewitt will naming "John Partain" as executor — an executorship, not a marriage, and not proof that this John Partain is even the same man as John A.
- A "1705 Surry will" sometimes used to identify this John Partin — a chronological impossibility if the ca.1722/25 birth estimate is used.

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence

No document in the recovered research corpus reports personal image-review of the will, the 1767 Hux deed, the 1762 or 1782 tax lists, or the 1793 Winifred deed. Every citation traces to a secondary AI-assisted research session or to the December 1997 NCGenWeb query. **The 1997 query is itself an important primary-type source for this project's own research history**: it is signed "Jim Partin," asks explicitly for John's parents, birthplace, marriage date, and Mary's maiden name, and reports John (b. ca.1722, d. Jul 1795), wife Mary (b. ca.1740, d. ca.1805), and the ten children listed above, plus the 1767 Hux purchase. **Because the submitter was explicitly seeking John's parents and Mary's maiden name, the query itself proves those identities were unresolved in this research tradition as of December 1997** — any later online tree that supplies precise parents or a maiden name for Mary must be independently sourced and cannot be back-projected onto this query as if it had always been known. The same "Jim Partin" is, per internal evidence, the author of the book-length *Barnabas Partin: Ancestors and Descendants, From Virginia to Mississippi and Beyond* recovered in September 2026 (see `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`) — a nearly thirty-year research effort by the same family researcher, cousin to the current project owner.

## Probate — What the 1795 Will Actually Requires

The 1795 will and its surrounding probate packet is the single highest-priority archival target in this entire project. Prior research established a systematic extraction protocol that should govern the eventual retrieval, reproduced here as a standing checklist rather than scattered across separate files:

**Do not rely on family-tree summaries.** The original or a high-quality image must be transcribed clause by clause, capturing separately: date written; date proved; residence; wife; every beneficiary and the *exact* relationship term used for each (son, daughter, cousin, witness, etc.); executors; witnesses; land descriptions; any enslaved persons named; livestock/tools/household goods; debts/notes; the residual clause; and signatures/marks.

**Do not stop at the will.** The full probate packet — probate order, executor bond, inventory, estate sale record, account current, final settlement, and any guardian bonds for minor children — can be more genealogically valuable than the will alone, since it will surface additional associates, in-laws, and neighbors (the "packet target matrix" developed in prior research specifically flagged Hux/Hucks, Fort, Rawlings, Tucker, Reeves, Lee, Moore, and Chatham as critical surnames to watch for across every document in the packet).

**Specific questions the full packet, once obtained, is capable of settling:** Is Mary called wife or widow specifically? Are Barnabas, David, Brasel/Brasel, Robert, and Charles explicitly called sons, or merely listed by name? Which daughters are named, and with what married surnames if any? Is the Great Darling Swamp tract specifically devised, and to whom? Does the Hux surname appear anywhere in the packet? Is David's 1797 tax district consistent with land he inherited?

## Contradictions — The Will's Contents

Three materially different accounts of the 1795 will's contents have been recovered, and this project does not adopt any one of them as settled:

1. **Version A (the working reading, most corroborated):** Sons Robert (Robin, executor), Brasel/Brasil, Barnabas, David, Charles; daughters Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, Elizabeth. Corroborated across the largest number of independent AI-assisted research sessions, the Opal App transcription log, and the externally authored Jim Partin book. Will page cited, inconsistently, as **Will Book 3, p.248** by most sources, **p.251** by one deterministic-scoring report, and **p.256** by a separate testator-index citation.
2. **Version B:** Sons "Bennet and Lewis" only, no daughters named — one narrative migration-research document, uncited, and inconsistent with every other source in the corpus.
3. **Version C:** A completely different heir set — daughters Sarah Applewhite (m. Henry Applewhite), Elizabeth Sullivant, Martha Brantley, Jane Bird; sons Robert Brantley and Thomas Brantley; daughter Lucy Russel (m. Richard Russel); daughter Elizabeth Jackson ("daughter of my last wife"); sons "John & Richard" ("sons of my last wife") — from a document that itself flags the Brantley/Jackson surnames as suggesting John Partin married a widow and raised stepchildren. Also gives the will's date as **2 January 1795**, rather than 13 July.

Version A is treated as this dossier's working reading given its broader corroboration and its independent support from the externally authored Jim Partin book — but Version C in particular cannot be dismissed without checking the primary image, since it could reflect a genuinely different Halifax testator conflated into this research thread by a derivative index. This is precisely why the will remains the project's top archival priority rather than a closed question.

## The Migration Bridge — Sussex VA to Halifax NC

No document recovered by this project supplies a direct bridging record — a deed, power of attorney, or explicit residence statement — connecting the Surry/Sussex, VA John Partin to the Halifax, NC John Partin. Every document that addresses this bridge explicitly says so, several using the phrase "ASSUMPTION CREEP DETECTED" as an internal self-warning. The circumstantial case rests entirely on: (a) John's disappearance from the Sussex tithable list between 1761 and 1763, roughly contemporary with (b) a John Partin's first appearance on the Halifax tax list in 1762, plus (c) the "landless heir" reasoning above for why no Virginia POA would necessarily exist even if this is the same man. **This remains this project's single most important unresolved identity bridge.**

## FAN Network

**William Reeves** (1762 tax-list associate, himself a Virginia migrant per several sources — the household under whom John A's earliest Halifax tax entry is listed). **William and Ann Hux** (1767 land grantors — the Hux family recurs across at least three later generations of this project's documented line: with William Partin in 1737 Surry, with John Partin in 1767 Halifax, and with Barnabas Partin's wife by the 1790s in South Carolina — a striking pattern requiring independent, non-circular verification at each stage; full detail in `10_FAN_NETWORKS/FAN_NETWORKS_DOSSIER.md`). **Elias Fort** (1772 grantee, himself reported as a Sussex/Surry VA migrant — proposed in prior research as a possible "conduit" figure for the broader VA→NC migration corridor, though this is explicitly circumstantial in every source that raises it; recovering his full 1772 deed, mapping his land, and reconstructing his own Virginia-to-Carolina migration remains an open research task). **Francis Floyd and son Benjamin Floyd** (1770 apprenticeship). For the complete Halifax neighborhood-reconstruction method — extracting every associate from tax lists, deeds, road orders, probate, and court minutes for every John Partin record 1762–1795, then prioritizing any name appearing in three or more independent record types — see `10_FAN_NETWORKS/FAN_NETWORKS_DOSSIER.md`.

## Great Darling Swamp — Geographic Anchor

The reported 1767 Hux-to-John-Partin tract adjoined Great Darlling/Darling Swamp. This remains a high-value geographic reconstruction target: every adjoining owner and nearby deed within a ten-year window of 1767 should be recovered (grantor and witness networks both, not only Partin entries), testing whether John was already occupying the land before the formal 1767 purchase, whether Hux or Fort held neighboring acreage, whether Virginia-origin FAN surnames appear nearby, whether the 1774 road order traverses this same neighborhood, and — most importantly — whether the 1795 will disposes of this same tract. Land continuity from 1767 through 1795 would help prove that the scattered Halifax records refer to one continuous John even without an explicit age statement anywhere in the record set.

## The 1790s Halifax Partin Population — A Warning Against Over-Merging

Search-index evidence surfaces **several** distinct Partin names in Halifax-era compiled census/tax-substitute material beyond John and his reported children — including a William Partin and a Benjamin Partin appearing independently in the 1780s–90s. **The late-eighteenth-century Halifax Partin population cannot safely be modeled as a single household.** Every indexed entry (John, William, Benjamin, David, and any Robert/Charles/Barnabas/Brasil entries) must be separated by district, neighbors, poll/acreage figures, exact date, and record type before any relationship is assumed. A "John Partin" index hit appearing after 1795 could easily be a different John and must not be used to either contradict or extend the reported July 1795 death without independent verification.

Modern public online-tree aggregations disagree sharply about this generation's John: some give a birth around 1745 with an entirely different child set; some attach him to "Mary Hewitt" (rejected above); some assign him to unrelated North Carolina Partin parents entirely; the 1997 Halifax query gives yet another reconstruction (b. ca.1722, wife Mary unknown). **This spread of mutually incompatible public trees is not evidence for any one pedigree — it demonstrates precisely why only primary probate, deed, and tax records can control this identification, and why no exact birth year, no maiden name for Mary, and no named parents for John should be imported from any public tree absent a cited primary record.**

## Post-1795 Household Dispersal

David Partin's 1797 appearance in the Halifax insolvent-tax list (District 2, one free poll, collector William Read) is this project's earliest documented post-1795 foothold. If the full probate packet eventually identifies David, Barnabas, Brasel, Robert, and Charles as sons/heirs, tracking each one's location from 1795–1810 should reveal how the estate household fragmented — Halifax stayers, South Carolina migrants (Barnabas, Brasel), and eventually Mississippi migrants (Barnabas) — through Halifax tax lists 1795–1810 for all Partin variants, deeds disposing of inherited John Partin land, estate distributions and guardian accounts, and the South Carolina and Mississippi records covered in this project's later dossiers. As with the 1790s population generally, **no Partin appearing in a 1790s Halifax index should be assumed to be John's child without a district-, land-, or probate-level relationship established.**

## David Partin Sr. — Halifax County, North Carolina (c.1780–1835)

David Partin Sr. is documented as a Halifax County taxpayer and landowner in the early nineteenth century, most concretely through a 4 December 1823 deed conveying his interest in land inherited from his father and, separately, "from the death of my mother," on a tract called "Robert Partin's tract." His parentage (commonly asserted as a son of John Partin, d. 1795, per the identically named son in Version A of the will above) rests on a single crowd-sourced online tree and is not established by any primary record recovered by this project. His wife's identity is the subject of five successive AI-assisted research drafts recovered from the project's Google Drive in September 2026 — and remains genuinely unresolved: the drafts do not converge toward an answer so much as cycle through one, discard it, and reassert it without engaging their own prior findings. This section exists specifically to document that evolution honestly, per this project's evidence standard, rather than presenting the most recent draft's confident language as settled fact.

### David — Land and Property

The 1823 deed's subject matter is itself the clearest land evidence: David held (and sold) an interest in land his father had held, and a separate interest inherited through his mother, on a tract known as "Robert Partin's tract" — a name that also surfaces independently in a separately recovered 1823 Halifax deed involving a different Partin grantor and in Robert "Robin" Partin's 1821 Darling Swamp deed to Rebecca Hux Partin, suggesting a family land tract that passed through multiple heirs of the 1795 testator — a connection this project has not yet proven with a title chain.

### David — Evolution of the Wife-Identity Investigation

Five successive AI-assisted drafts of the same question — "who was David Partin Sr.'s wife?" — were recovered. Read in sequence, they show the confidence of the answer rising even as the underlying evidence does not change:

1. **Draft 1** ("David Partin Sr." field worksheet). Bare methodology and a list of records to pull. Offers no conclusion. Establishes the 1823 deed as the strongest lead and explicitly warns against leaping from a disputed word in that deed to any specific name: "any leap from Kurson? to Cousins/Keziah without corroboration" is flagged as invalid.
2. **Draft 2** ("Identifying The Wife of David Partin Sr."). The most disciplined draft in the series. States plainly and repeatedly what the evidence does *not* support: Keziah as a proven given name, Revelle or Hawkins as a proven maiden family, or the 1823 deed as containing any actual wife's signature or dower release. Final verdict: **"IDENTITY UNRESOLVED."**
3. **Draft 3** ("David Partin Sr.'s Wife Identification"). A methodological regression. Introduces the WikiTree-sourced parentage claim (John Partin as father, birth year 1780), a "Deed Book 26" citation for the 1823 deed not found in the more careful drafts, and a District 2 neighbor survey (Hawkins, Lane, Joyner families). Despite acknowledging the wife's name is "not stated in a single, surviving marriage bond," its concluding synthesis asserts she "was a member of the Revelle or Hawkins family, and likely carried the name Keziah" — a direct reversal of Draft 2's explicit prohibition on exactly this claim.
4. **Draft 4** ("Identifying David Partin Sr.'s Wife"). Adds a formal paleographic transcription exercise on the disputed word in the 1823 deed's inheritance clause (which names David's *mother*, not his wife — a conflation Draft 2 had specifically warned against). This draft reads the word as "Kursin," proposes a "Kirstin" (Scottish form of Christina) hypothesis, and states it has "definitively" ruled out "Keziah" as the deed's actual word — but supplies no image, no archival citation, and no completed letterform comparison to support that conclusion; the comparison it proposes as the actual verification step is explicitly left undone.
5. **Draft 5** ("Forensic Genealogical Analysis of the Marital Identity of David Partin Sr...."). The most recent and most polished draft. Silently drops Draft 4's "Kursin" finding entirely — it is not mentioned, engaged, or refuted — and reverts to Draft 3's Keziah/Revelle/Hawkins framing, stated in the most declarative language of the whole series: "the wife of David Partin Sr. is identified as a daughter of the local kinship network in District 2, likely of the Revelle or Hawkins lineage."

**Current status: UNRESOLVED.** The only claim that survives all five drafts with a consistent, real citation is the 1823 deed's existence and content, which does not name a wife at all. No draft reports having located, opened, or quoted from: (a) a deed where David Partin Sr.'s wife is named as a joining grantor or dower-releaser, (b) a tax-list widow entry tied to David, (c) an administration or estate record naming his widow, or (d) the "Partin Family Bible" that every draft names as the record that would resolve this question.

## Identity Proof Framework — How This Project Tests "Same Man" Claims Generally

Two separate proof problems recur throughout this dossier and must never be collapsed into one: (1) whether the various Halifax-era John Partin records (tax, deed, road, probate, 1762–1795) all describe the same internally continuous individual, and (2) whether that Halifax individual is the same man as the Surry/Sussex Virginia John Partin. Proving (1) requires testing geography (same tract/watershed/neighbors), FAN network (Hux, Fort, and recurring witnesses), chronology (no conflicting simultaneous John households), property continuity (acquisition, taxation, road duty, eventual devise), and family continuity (names in probate matching earlier deeds/taxes). Proving (2) is an entirely separate question requiring an actual Virginia-to-Carolina bridging document, not merely the internal consistency of the Halifax record set.

## Evidence Assessment (John A, whole dossier)

- **STRONG:** Halifax residency from 1762; land acquisition and tax presence through the 1780s; death and probate in 1795.
