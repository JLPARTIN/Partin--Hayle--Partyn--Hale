# Rejected and Quarantined Claims

Claims that have been tested against the recovered research corpus and found unsupported, actively contradicted, or the product of evidence-inflation. Each entry states the claim's origin, the evidence for and against it, the current verdict, and what would reopen it. Nothing here is deleted from project memory — quarantining a claim preserves the project's research history and prevents the same error from being re-introduced.

---

## 1. Margaret Partin's maiden name was Hayle

**Origin of claim:** A long-standing working hypothesis in this project (see prior versions of `01_ENGLAND_THAMES/LONDON_NETWORK.md`), proposed to explain Thomas Hayle's presence in Robert and Margaret Partin's Virginia household.

**Evidence for:** None resting on a primary record recovered by this project.

**Evidence against:** The recovered 1625/26 will of Symon Hayle, the London tailor proposed elsewhere in this project as Thomas Hayle's father, names his children explicitly (son Thomas, contingent heirs son Robert and daughter Anne) and does not include a daughter Margaret.

**Current verdict: REJECTED pending new evidence.**

**What could reopen it:** Discovery that Margaret belonged to a different, unreconstructed branch of the Hayle family; or an independent primary record (a marriage bond, a will, a deposition) directly stating Margaret's maiden name as Hayle.

## 2. John Partin's (d. 1795, Halifax Co. NC) wife's maiden name was Hewitt

**Origin of claim:** Traceable in the recovered corpus to a single WikiTree page (Partin-664), repeated uncritically across several derivative AI-assisted research documents, including a specific claimed marriage date of 1766.

**Evidence for:** None beyond the WikiTree page and documents citing it.

**Evidence against:** A dedicated investigation in the recovered corpus conducted a targeted sweep of Halifax and Edgecombe County Hewitt/Huet probate records and found zero primary evidence of a Mary Partin as a daughter or legatee in any Hewitt estate, concluding the claim is likely a "Downstream Hallucination or a conflation with a later generation."

**Current verdict: REJECTED.** The wife should be recorded as "Mary [Unknown]" until a primary record establishes her maiden name.

**What could reopen it:** A primary marriage bond, deed of gift, or will clause directly naming Mary's family of origin.

---

## 3. The 1746 Surry County, Virginia estate payment to "John Partin" is a "Smoking Gun" proving father-son heirship between Robert Partin and John Partin

**Origin of claim:** The project owner's own Jamestowne Society application-support draft ("PARTIN DIRECT LINE"), styled as advocacy for a lineage submission rather than a neutral audit.

**Evidence for:** The payment entry itself is real (though its exact amount is separately disputed — see Contradiction 5 in `08_SOURCES/SOURCE_CONTRADICTIONS/MASTER_CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md`) and does place a "John Partin" as a recipient from a Robert Partin estate.

**Evidence against:** At least four independently recovered documents — including both a Gemini and a ChatGPT run of a dedicated parentage investigation, and a long-form self-auditing transcript — state plainly that the payment record **does not use the word "son"** and is inferential only. The self-auditing transcript separately calls this exact bridge "the biggest problem" in the entire research program and states there is "no primary state-line bridge record."

**Current verdict: REJECTED as a "Smoking Gun" characterization; the underlying record remains a legitimate WORKING HYPOTHESIS lead, not proof.** This project's dossiers should describe the 1746 payment as circumstantial support for possible heirship, never as documentary proof of paternity.

**What could reopen it (as proof, not merely as a lead):** Direct image review of the original 1746 account, and/or an independent document using explicit filiation language ("my son John," "John, son of Robert").

## 4. "Brick•Wall•Broke" establishes the Virginia-to-North-Carolina Robert-to-John generational bridge

**Origin of claim:** A ~130,000-character AI chat-log document of that title, recovered from the project's Google Drive, framed throughout as having achieved a breakthrough on this question.

**Evidence for:** The document repeatedly asserts high confidence, using language such as "rock-solid" and "beyond reasonable doubt."

**Evidence against:** The document's own text repeatedly and explicitly admits, in at least four separate places, that "no document explicitly states 'John son of Robert.'" It also contains unreconciled internal numeric contradictions (Margaret's muster age given as both 56 and 36 within the same document; Robert's age given as both 30 and 36) and a same-name conflation risk, where a debt-attached "adult John Partin" in a 1726 record is ambiguously treated as both the target ancestor (who would have been a toddler at that date under the document's own chronology) and as his own father.

**Current verdict: REJECTED as a proof document.** It is retained in the project's source inventory as a research-history artifact and as a cautionary example of evidence-inflation (escalating confidence language without new evidence between claims), not as a citable finding.

**What could reopen it:** None of its own content — only new, independently verified primary records could establish the bridge it claims to have already established.

## 5. David Partin Sr.'s wife was named Keziah, of the Revelle or Hawkins family

**Origin of claim:** The most recent of five successive AI-assisted drafts investigating this question (see `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/DAVID_PARTIN_SR_DOSSIER.md` for the full evolution).

**Evidence for:** An 1823 deed in which David conveys land inherited "from the death of my mother," on a tract called "Robert Partin's tract" — but this deed does not itself name the mother, let alone a wife, and its disputed key word has been read three incompatible ways across the drafts (unaddressed; supporting "Keziah"; explicitly read as "Kursin," not Keziah).

**Evidence against:** An earlier, more disciplined draft in the same series explicitly and repeatedly states the evidence does **not** support Keziah as a proven given name, Revelle or Hawkins as a proven maiden family, or the 1823 deed as containing any wife's signature or dower release. A still-later draft explicitly and "definitively" rejects "Keziah" as the deed's actual word — a rejection the most recent, most confidently worded draft simply ignores rather than refutes.

**Current verdict: UNRESOLVED, not REJECTED** — this claim has not been affirmatively disproven the way Claims 1 and 2 above have; it has simply never been proven, despite five attempts, and its most recent statement overstates what its own citations support. It is listed here as a caution against treating the most recent or most confidently worded draft as the most reliable one.

**What could reopen/resolve it:** Any of the four record types every draft names as the missing proof and none reports having found: a deed naming David's wife as joining grantor or dower-releaser; a tax-list widow entry; an administration record naming a widow; or the "Partin Family Bible" referenced throughout the series but never actually quoted from.

## 6. "John Partin, wife Winifred" is confirmed as the son of John Partin (d. 1795)

**Origin of claim:** Stated as settled fact, without hedge, in one recovered reasoning/chronology document, and inconsistently labeled "CONFIRMED — Updated" in the summary table of a separate structured registry (whose own explanatory prose in the same document calls the same relationship "a well-supported working hypothesis... does not yet meet the full GPS standard of proof").

**Evidence for:** Concurrent migration timing with Barnabas Partin (a documented son of John, d.1795) to the same South Carolina district; the 1795 will possibly naming a son John (via a disputed duplicate "Robert" legacy line some documents read as a scribal error for "John"); general Hux-family title-chain overlap.

**Evidence against:** A separate, dedicated identity-ledger document states this relationship's parentage is flatly **"Unknown... requires independent primary source investigation,"** and explicitly labels every one of its own four core supporting records "Not-Image-Viewed."

**Current verdict: REJECTED as a confirmed fact; retained as an active WORKING HYPOTHESIS.** No dossier in this project should state John B's parentage as John A's son without this hedge.

**What could reopen it:** A Halifax deed-liquidation search, 1795–1802, for John Partin selling Halifax land — identified by multiple sources as the specific record that would close this question either way.

## 7. Robert Partin Sr.'s English origin was Bristol/Somerset/Gloucestershire

**Origin of claim:** One recovered document, offered with no archival citation, only the word "likely." **Confirmed directly by the project owner (Oct 2026 session) as one of several unsourced working theories produced while manually untangling a large number of conflicting, uncited online family trees** — not a research lead with any documentary basis. The owner's own account: this project's underlying research corpus is contradiction-heavy precisely because it records that process — working through bad online-tree claims toward source-backed information, done across many separate, unorganized AI-assisted sessions rather than in one running document. See `RESEARCH_RECOVERY_LOG.md`.

**Evidence for:** None. None found in the recovered corpus beyond the assertion itself, and the owner has confirmed no such evidence exists in their own working notes either.

**Evidence against:** A separately recovered, more specifically cited document places the family in London, citing London Metropolitan Archives references for a 1586 marriage (St. Botolph Aldgate) and a 1589 baptism (St. Margaret Pattens). This project's `RESEARCH_STATUS.md` has separately and independently warned against treating "St. Mary Somerset" (a London parish name attached to the Hayle family) as evidence of a Somerset-county or Bristol origin — the Bristol/Gloucestershire theory appears to be exactly that conflation, mistaking the London parish name "St. Mary Somerset" for a reference to Somerset county or nearby Bristol.

**Current verdict: REJECTED outright, per the project owner's own confirmation that this was an unsourced working theory, not a documented lead.** It should not be repeated or re-tested as a candidate origin absent a genuine new primary citation. **The St. Mary Somerset, London research thread (Hayle family parish, and the Partyn/Partin candidate research tied to the same London network) is explicitly retained as the project's legitimate, source-backed English-origins thread** — see `01_ENGLAND_THAMES/LONDON_NETWORK.md`. The competing London citations for Robert Partin Sr. specifically (St. Botolph Aldgate 1586 marriage; St. Margaret Pattens 1589 baptism) remain themselves not yet independently image-verified by this project and should be treated as CANDIDATE, not proven — but they are the only origin thread this project should continue developing.

**What could reopen a Bristol/Gloucestershire theory:** An actual archival citation (parish register, will, or other original record) naming a Bristol/Gloucestershire origin — none currently exists, and none should be assumed while pursuing the London thread.
