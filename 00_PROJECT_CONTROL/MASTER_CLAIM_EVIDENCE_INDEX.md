# Master Claim / Evidence Index

The forensic backbone of the project. Every major genealogical proposition recovered from the research corpus (see `RECOVERED_RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md`) is entered here once, with its evidence, contradictory evidence, reasoning, and current status. Status labels follow `EVIDENCE_STANDARD.md`: **PROVEN / STRONG / WORKING HYPOTHESIS / CANDIDATE / REJECTED / UNRESOLVED**.

A claim is never silently upgraded here. Where the recovered corpus itself disagrees about a claim's strength, that disagreement is recorded rather than adjudicated away.

---

## CLAIM 1 — Robert Partin Sr. arrived in Virginia in June 1609 aboard the *Blessing*

**STATUS: STRONG**

**Evidence:** The 1624/25 Virginia Muster (a genuine, identifiable primary record type — "List of the Living in Virginia," James City/West & Shirley Hundred) is reported across multiple independent research sessions as recording Robert Partin, age ~36, with an arrival note tying him to the *Blessing*, June 1609. The muster additionally lists wife Margaret and children.

**Source quality:** The muster itself is a primary record. No document in the recovered corpus reports having personally viewed the muster's original image or a specific folio/page number for the arrival-ship notation; every citation is to a secondary transcription or abstract (Virtual Jamestown database, *Cavaliers and Pioneers*).

**Contradictory evidence:** None on the core fact. One document (the "Partin Identity Ledger," Category A) gives the 1624/25 muster wife's name as "Rebecca" rather than "Margaret" — this is very likely a compiler error confusing the wife with a daughter named Rebecca who appears elsewhere in the same corpus, and is flagged, not treated as a competing fact.

**Research needed:** Direct examination of the muster's original manuscript image or the National Archives (UK), CO 1/3 reference cited by one source, to confirm the exact page/folio and the precise wording linking Robert Partin to the *Blessing*.

## CLAIM 2 — Robert Partin Sr. married a woman named Margaret

**STATUS: STRONG**

**Evidence:** Consistent across nearly every document in the corpus that the 1624/25 muster names "Margaret Partin" as Robert's wife, age given variously as 36 or 56 depending on the document (an unresolved internal numeric discrepancy — see Contradiction Register).

**Research needed:** Original muster image, to resolve the age discrepancy and confirm no maiden name is given in the muster itself (none of the recovered documents claim the muster states a maiden name).

## CLAIM 3 — Margaret Partin's maiden name was Hayle

**STATUS: REJECTED**

**Evidence for:** None found in the recovered corpus that rests on a primary record. The "Hayle network" working hypothesis (long-standing in this project's prior sessions, see `01_ENGLAND_THAMES/LONDON_NETWORK.md`) proposed this connection as a way to explain Thomas Hayle's presence in Robert Partin's household.

**Evidence against:** The single strongest primary-source find in the entire recovered corpus is a transcription of the 1625/26 London will of **Symon Hayle**, tailor, of the parish of St. Mary Somerset — the man proposed elsewhere in the project as Thomas Hayle's father. The will names Symon's children explicitly (son Thomas, contingent heirs son Robert Hayle and daughter Anne Hayle) and his wife/executrix Elizabeth Hayle. **It does not name a daughter Margaret.** The recovered document analyzing this will states this directly: "Symon Hayle's will does not list a daughter named Margaret. This confirms that Margaret Partin's maiden name is likely not Hayle, necessitating a search for alternative origins for the Partin matriarch."

**Reasoning:** A will that names surviving/contingent children and does not include a Margaret is meaningful negative evidence against Margaret Partin having been Symon Hayle's daughter — though it does not rule out Margaret having belonged to a different branch of the Hayle family, or having died/married out before the will was written.

**Verdict:** The Hayle-maiden-name hypothesis for Margaret Partin should be treated as **REJECTED pending new evidence**, not merely unproven. This directly downgrades the project's prior "Working Hypothesis — Hayle Connection" framing in `LONDON_NETWORK.md`, which should now be revised to state the aunt/nephew (not sibling, not mother/son) hypothesis discussed under Claim 5 below as the strongest surviving form of a Hayle-Partin kinship theory — and that even *that* hypothesis explains Thomas Hayle's presence via some other, unidentified relationship to Margaret, not via Margaret being Symon's daughter.

**Research needed:** Full reconstruction of the Hayle family beyond Symon's immediate household; search for a marriage record of a Margaret Hayle from a different branch; search for a completely non-Hayle origin for Margaret.

## CLAIM 4 — Thomas Hayle was executed in 1627 for a sexual offense against a child of Robert and Margaret Partin

**STATUS: PROVEN**

**Evidence:** A verbatim transcription of a June 1627 Virginia General Court record was recovered, reading in relevant part: *"This Court take into consideracon the full and p[er]ticular informacon of God given unto it p[er]ticularly of the offense and abominable and damnable fact of Thomas Hayle committed upon Avis Partin the daughter of Robert Partin and Margarett his wife, and upon three other maiden children, and finding him guilty of the rape & Ravishment of the said foure maiden children, doe therefore order & adiudge that the said Thomas Hayle be hanged by the neck untill he be dead..."* The record further orders co-defendant Charles Maxey (separately convicted of a similar offense) to serve as Hayle's executioner and then be whipped at James City and Shirley Hundred, and directs one-third of Hayle's forfeited estate to Avis Partin.

This independently corroborates — and gives the specific victim's name and offense for — the previously known court record (already in `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/GENERAL_COURT_1627.md`) describing Margaret Partin's separate prosecution for concealing Thomas Hayle's offense.

**Source quality:** This reads as a genuine primary-source transcription (period spelling, abbreviation marks, and bracketed uncertain readings are preserved in the recovered document) rather than a modern paraphrase. It has not been independently checked by this project against the actual Virginia General Court manuscript or a scholarly printed edition (e.g., *Virginia Magazine of History and Biography*).

**What this record does NOT prove:** It does not state or imply any kinship between Thomas Hayle and the Partin family — it identifies Thomas Hayle only as the offender and Avis Partin only as a victim. Any relationship between Thomas Hayle and Margaret Partin (aunt/nephew, or otherwise) is a separate, distinct hypothesis (see Claim 5) resting on other evidence, not on this record.

**Research needed:** Independent verification against the original Virginia General Court manuscript or an authoritative printed edition; confirmation of Avis Partin's exact relationship (daughter vs. another status) and age at the time.

## CLAIM 5 — Thomas Hayle (the 1624/25 muster servant, executed 1627) was the son of Symon Hayle, a London tailor, making him Margaret Partin's nephew rather than her brother

**STATUS: WORKING HYPOTHESIS**

**Evidence:** Chronological fit — the 1624/25 Virginia Muster records Thomas Hayle as "aged 20 years"; Symon Hayle's will (dated Feb 1625/26) sets his son Thomas's inheritance to vest at age 21, consistent with Thomas approaching majority shortly before his 1627 execution and his colonial estate being forfeited rather than collected. One recovered document (the Hayle_Partin_Genealogical_Proof_Statement) separately argues, from London Metropolitan Archives parish-register citations for St. Mary Somerset, that Margaret Hayle and Thomas Hayle's baptismal generation gap is consistent with an aunt/nephew relationship rather than siblings, and explicitly identifies and rejects an uncited online-tree database as the source of a previously circulated "siblings" claim.

**Reasoning is explicitly inferential, not a documented statement:** No recovered document produces a record that directly states "Thomas Hayle, son of Symon Hayle" in a Virginia context, or any document connecting the London Symon Hayle household to the Virginia Thomas Hayle beyond the age/inheritance-timing coincidence.

**Contradictory/complicating evidence:** Given Claim 3's rejection of the Hayle maiden name for Margaret, this hypothesis currently has no documented mechanism connecting Margaret Partin to the Hayle family at all — the aunt/nephew framing was originally built on the assumption that Margaret was herself a Hayle. Absent that, Thomas Hayle's presence in Robert and Margaret Partin's Virginia household remains **unexplained by any kinship theory currently supported by a primary record.**

**Research needed:** An independent, non-Hayle-maiden-name explanation for why Thomas Hayle appears as a servant/household member with the Partins; confirmation that the Virginia Thomas Hayle and the London Symon Hayle's son Thomas are the same individual (no direct document makes this connection; it rests entirely on age arithmetic).

## CLAIM 6 — Robert Partin Jr. was the son of Robert Partin Sr. ("the elder")

**STATUS: STRONG**

**Evidence:** Multiple independent documents in the corpus quote a 1642 Virginia deed (recorded 1645) using the explicit phrase **"Robert Partin, the elder, and Robert Partin, the younger, his sonn"**, conveying 100 acres in Shirley Hundred to Daniel Lluellin/Lewellin. A companion 1655 patent is separately quoted using "Senr." and "Junr." designations for the same pair. This is the single clearest direct-kinship documentary language recovered anywhere in the corpus for any generational link in the entire Partin line.

**Source quality:** Cited to Nugent, *Cavaliers and Pioneers*, Vol. 1 (pp. 165, 317 per one citation) — a real, checkable published abstract of Virginia land patents, though not itself the original patent image.

**Research needed:** Confirm the exact patent-book/page citation against Nugent's published volume and, ideally, the Library of Virginia's original patent register.

## CLAIM 7 — Robert Partin (Surry Co. VA, d. 1743/44) was survived by wife Elizabeth Rawlings, later wife Mary

**STATUS: STRONG, with an unresolved internal contradiction**

**Evidence:** The best-cited single document in the entire corpus ("Partin_Surry_County_Records_Compiled-1," 16 fully FamilySearch-ARK-cited transcriptions) shows a Robert Partin's wife named **Elizabeth** in a 1727 dower-relinquishment deed (Robert signs by mark: "Robert R Partin — [his] Marke"), and a **different-named wife, Mary**, granted Letters of Administration for "the Estate of Robert Partin deced" in late 1746, with an estate account (20 Aug 1746) separately administered by court-appointed Robert Connell rather than Mary. The same compiler explicitly flags both the Elizabeth/Mary discrepancy and the Mary-administratrix-vs-Connell-administrator discrepancy as unreconciled, rather than resolving them.

Separately, John Rawlings's own 1702/03 will is reported to name his daughter using the exact abbreviated original wording **"Eliza. Partin"** — preserved here in its original form per project practice, since "Eliza." is evidentially distinct from a modernized "Elizabeth."

**Reasoning:** Two explanations remain open per the compiling document itself: two successive wives (Elizabeth predeceasing, Robert remarrying Mary), or a naming/transcription variance. Neither is adopted here as fact.

**Research needed:** Direct image review of the 1727 dower deed and 1746 administration/estate-account records; search for an intervening death record or marriage record for either Elizabeth or Mary.

## CLAIM 8 — This Surry County Robert Partin (d. 1743/44) was the father of "John Partin" who later appears as the Halifax Co., NC patriarch (d. 1795)

**STATUS: UNRESOLVED — actively and explicitly disputed within the recovered corpus itself**

**Evidence for:** A 1746 Surry Co. estate account entry paying "John Partin" a sum from the deceased Robert Partin's estate — reported with materially different figures across documents: **"592" (unit unstated, presumably pounds of tobacco)** in one source, versus **"3 Pounds, 15 Shillings"** in another, for what both describe as the same transaction. Several documents in the corpus (including the project owner's own Jamestowne Society application draft, "PARTIN DIRECT LINE") treat this payment as a "Smoking Gun"/definitive proof of heirship.

**Evidence against / explicit rejection of that confidence level:** At least four independent documents in the corpus — including the project's own most methodologically rigorous triage document and both the Gemini and ChatGPT parallel runs of a dedicated parentage investigation — state plainly that this payment **does not say "son John"** and is inferential only. One document states verbatim: *"The 1746 payment to John Partin from Robert's estate is strong but does not state 'son John.'"* The dedicated audit transcript in the corpus independently calls the Robert-III/IV-to-John bridge "the biggest problem" in the entire research program and states there is "no primary state-line bridge record." A separate document explicitly classifies the whole class of 1742–1746 Surry "inheritance" claims as "unstable... unresolved claim pending original retrieval," directly contradicting a different document in the same corpus that calls a related 1742 deed "GATE-PASSED... an extraordinary rarity."

**Reasoning:** The recovered corpus contains a direct, internal, and never-resolved disagreement about how strong this evidence is. The more advocacy-styled documents (built toward a lineage-society submission) treat the link as proven; the more neutral, self-checking documents do not. Per this project's evidence standard, the more conservative reading governs until a primary record is produced that actually states the filial relationship.

**Verdict:** **WORKING HYPOTHESIS at best**, not proven. The amount discrepancy (592 vs. 3£15s) must also be resolved against the primary image before this record can be cited with confidence at all.

**Research needed:** Direct image review of the 1746 Surry Co. estate account (FamilySearch image cited by one source as ARK 3Q9M-C9PX-K6KN); resolution of the amount/units discrepancy; a search for any document using explicit filiation language ("son John," "my son John") connecting a Surry Robert Partin to a Halifax John Partin.

## CLAIM 9 — John Partin (Halifax Co., NC) died in 1795, will naming wife Mary and multiple children

**STATUS: STRONG for the bare fact of death/probate; UNRESOLVED for the will's exact contents**

**Evidence:** Nearly every document in the recovered corpus that addresses a 1795 Halifax County will agrees a John Partin died in Halifax Co., NC, will proved in 1795, naming wife "Mary" and children including Robert (executor) and Barnabas. This is the single most stable anchor identity in the whole corpus.

**Contradictory evidence — the will's contents are reported at least three incompatible ways:**
1. Sons Robert, Brasel/Brasil, Barnabas, David, Charles; daughters Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, Elizabeth (the version corroborated by the largest number of independent documents, including a direct transcription abstract, and by the Jim Partin book's account of John's 1795 will).
2. Sons "Bennet and Lewis" only, no daughters named (one narrative-synthesis document, uncited).
3. A completely different heir set — daughters Sarah Applewhite, Elizabeth Sullivant, Martha Brantley, Jane Bird, Lucy Russel, Elizabeth Jackson; sons Robert Brantley, Thomas Brantley, and "John & Richard" described as "sons of my last wife" — from a narrative document that itself flags the Brantley/Jackson surnames as suggesting John Partin married a widow and raised stepchildren.

The exact page citation for this will is also given at least three ways across the corpus: **Will Book 3, page 248**; **Will Book 3, page 251**; and **Will Book 3, page 256** (this last figure from a different testator index citation). The exact date is given as variously **13 July 1795 (proved August 1795)**, and **2 January 1795**.

**Reasoning:** No document in the recovered corpus reports having personally viewed the will's original image. Version 1 above is corroborated by the largest number of independently-run research sessions and by the externally authored Jim Partin book, and should be treated as the current working reading — but it is not proven, and versions 2 and 3 cannot be dismissed without checking the primary image, since one of them could reflect a genuinely different testator being conflated into this file.

**Research needed:** This is the single highest-priority archival target in the entire Halifax Co. NC research program: direct image review of Halifax Co. Will Book 3 (FamilySearch DGS 4755347 per one citation) at every page number given above, to establish the correct page and the correct, complete heir list once and for all.

## CLAIM 10 — John Partin's (d. 1795) wife's maiden name was Hewitt ("Mary Hewitt" / "Mary Francis Hewitt Hux")

**STATUS: REJECTED**

**Evidence for:** Repeated in several derivative documents, ultimately traceable to a single WikiTree page.

**Evidence against:** A document specifically dedicated to testing this claim ("John Partin Research: Hewitt Anomaly") conducted a targeted sweep of Halifax/Edgecombe Hewitt/Huet probate records and found **zero** primary evidence of a Mary Partin as a daughter or legatee in any Hewitt estate, concluding the claim is likely a "Downstream Hallucination or a conflation with a later generation," and locking the wife's identity as **"Mary [Unknown]"** pending a primary marriage bond or deed of gift. A separate, independently run migration-research document reaches the same negative conclusion.

**Verdict:** REJECTED pending new evidence. This directly contradicts other documents in the same corpus that state the Hewitt marriage (with a specific date of 1766) as settled fact — those documents should be understood as having repeated an unverified derivative claim.

**Research needed:** A genuine primary marriage bond or deed of gift naming John Partin's wife by maiden name, if one survives.

## CLAIM 11 — There were (at least) three separate men named "John Partin" active in the Surry/Sussex VA → Halifax NC corridor in the mid-to-late 1700s ("the Three Johns")

**STATUS: STRONG as a methodological framework; individual identities within it remain CANDIDATE/WORKING HYPOTHESIS**

**Evidence:** This is the one point of near-total agreement across the recovered corpus. Multiple independently run documents converge on separating:
- **"John A"** — the 1795 Halifax testator, wife Mary (see Claims 9–10).
- **"John B"** — a John Partin/Parton with wife **Winifred**, documented in a 30 Jan 1793 Halifax deed and a 24 Feb / 4 Mar 1802 Kershaw District, SC deed and dower-release pair.
- **"John C"** — an Orange County, NC collateral John Partin, son of a different William Partin (will proved 1804), explicitly unconnected to A or B.

Every document that discusses more than one of these individuals states they must not be merged absent explicit proof.

**What is NOT agreed, and remains a live internal contradiction:** whether "John B" (Winifred) is a **son** of "John A." This is stated with three different confidence levels by three different recovered documents: **"Unknown... requires independent primary source investigation"**; **"is the son of John #1"** asserted as settled fact; and **"a well-supported working hypothesis... does not yet meet the full GPS standard of proof"** — this last document's own summary table separately (and inconsistently) labels the same relationship "CONFIRMED — Updated," an internal self-contradiction within a single source.

One document additionally proposes that the 1795 will's reported duplicate "son Robert" legacy line may be a scribal error for "son John," which — if true — would itself constitute the missing direct link. This is explicitly labeled speculative by its own author and is not corroborated elsewhere.

**Verdict:** The Three Johns *separation* is well-supported and should be treated as the project's working framework. The claim that John B is John A's *son* remains **UNRESOLVED**, not proven, regardless of how confidently any single source states it.

**Research needed:** Direct image review of the 1793 Halifax deed and 1795 will; a targeted Halifax deed-liquidation search 1795–1802 for John Partin selling Halifax land, which every document proposing the John-A/John-B link identifies as the actual proof that would close this question.

## CLAIM 13 — Barnabas Partin's birth was 29 Aug 1773 and death was 24 Mar 1855

**STATUS: WORKING HYPOTHESIS — internally contradicted by the same primary-adjacent source material**

**Evidence:** The Jim Partin book states both dates as fact in its Barnabas Partin entry, citing Wilkinson Co. MS census, tax, land, and probate records (Probate Wills Book 2, p. 100; Probate Minutes Vol. 9, pp. 366–367).

**Contradictory evidence, preserved rather than resolved:**
- *Birth:* the book's own cited *Woodville Republican* obituary states Barnabas's age at death as "82 years, 7 months," implying a birth of roughly **August 1772** — about a year off from the stated 1773. A separately cited *Fort Adams News* obituary gives his age as "86 years," implying a birth of roughly **1769** — about four years off.
- *Death:* the book gives 24 March 1855; the same *Fort Adams News* obituary states he died "the 25th," i.e., **25 March 1855** — a one-day conflict with the book's own stated date.

**Verdict:** Per this project's standard, both obituary variants are preserved alongside the book's stated dates rather than silently harmonized. The 24 Mar 1855 death date and 29 Aug 1773 birth date should be treated as the current working dates (best sourced, most internally consistent with the book's own narrative), with the obituary variants flagged as an open discrepancy.

**Research needed:** Original obituary clippings if they survive; Wilkinson Co. probate file for an exact death date.

## CLAIM 14 — Barnabas Partin's wife Margaret "Peggy" was a member of the Hux family

**STATUS: WORKING HYPOTHESIS**

**Evidence:** Circumstantial but multiply convergent: William Hux's Kershaw Co., SC land (granted 1786) is repeatedly described in deed narrations as bordering "Barney Partin's land"; the Jim Partin book narrates (without a book/page citation) a 6 March 1811 Kershaw District, SC deed in which Barnabas and "Peggy" sold 353 acres to Thomas English with Peggy relinquishing dower; a 1793 Halifax Co. marriage bond date (15 March 1793) for Barnabas Partin and Margaret "Peggy" Hux is cited in multiple independent documents; and a 25-45 cM autosomal DNA cluster to Hux-surnamed testers from the Kershaw SC 1780s-1800s cluster is reported in one document (unverified independently by this project).

**Critical gap:** The single deed most directly probative of Margaret's Hux origin — the 6 March 1811 dower-relinquishment deed — is narrated in detail by the Jim Partin book but **without a deed book or page citation anywhere in the recovered corpus.** This is flagged as the highest-priority single archival target for this specific question.

**Research needed:** Locate the 6 March 1811 Kershaw/Camden District, SC deed book and page; a primary marriage record for the 1793 Halifax bond.

## CLAIM 15 — David Partin Sr. of Halifax Co., NC (fl. c.1795–1835) was a son of John Partin (d. 1795) and his wife's identity was Keziah, of the Revelle or Hawkins family

**STATUS: UNRESOLVED — see `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/DAVID_PARTIN_SR_DOSSIER.md` for full evolution-of-investigation detail**

**Evidence for parentage:** Sourced in every drafted document exclusively to a single WikiTree page (Partin-664); no primary Halifax record in the recovered corpus directly names David's parents.

**Evidence for wife's identity:** None that withstands scrutiny. Five successive AI-drafted investigations of this exact question exist in the corpus; the only fact that survives all five with a primary-type citation is a 4 December 1823 deed (David Parton to Thomas Revelle, DGS 007547227, FamilySearch image 629 of 774) in which David conveys land inherited through his father and "from the death of my mother," on a tract called "Robert Partin's tract" — this deed does not name the mother, let alone a wife. A later draft "definitively" read the deed's disputed mother's-name word as "Kursin"/"Kirstin" rather than "Keziah," but supplied no image or letterform comparison to support that reading; the final, most polished draft in the sequence silently reverts to asserting "Keziah... of the Revelle or Hawkins lineage" as the conclusion, without engaging or refuting the intervening rejection.

**Verdict:** Both the parentage claim and the wife-identity claim are UNRESOLVED. No draft in the series reports having located, opened, or quoted a deed naming David's wife, a tax-list widow entry, an administration record naming a widow, or the "Partin Family Bible" that every draft names as the needed next step.

**Research needed:** All of the above, still outstanding.

## CLAIM 16 — Y-DNA testing proves direct paternal descent from the 1609 Virginia immigrant Robert Partin

**STATUS: NOT SUPPORTED AS STATED — genetic fact and genealogical claim must be kept separate**

**Evidence:** A project-owner-administered Y-DNA test (kit MK85913) is reported as an exact match (0 genetic distance, 700 markers) to other FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project testers who have entered "Robert Partin b. 1655 England" as their own paternal-line ancestor, under terminal SNP R-FTF47086.

**What this does and does not establish:** A shared, very-low-distance Y-STR/SNP match among testers establishes that those testers share a genuinely recent common paternal ancestor. It does **not**, by itself, establish that the shared ancestor is any specific *named* historical individual — that identification rests entirely on each tester's own (documentary, not genetic) family-tree entry, which is itself unverified genealogical opinion, not laboratory fact. This project's own DNA framework states the same principle independently.

**Additional finding:** The raw 19-row Y-STR/haplogroup spreadsheet recovered from the project's Google Drive (haplogroups R-M269, R-FTE30394, R-BY31280, R-FTB85652, R-F22409, R-Y32699, clustering in Tennessee/North Carolina/South Carolina) does **not** contain a row for terminal SNP R-FTF47086 or kit MK85913 — meaning this project's own two DNA data sources cannot currently be cross-referenced against each other.

**Research needed:** Obtain kit MK85913's full results and terminal-SNP placement directly from FamilyTreeDNA; determine its relationship (if any) to the R-FTE30394 cluster; treat every participant-entered "earliest known ancestor" as a claim to be documented, not evidence to be relied upon.
