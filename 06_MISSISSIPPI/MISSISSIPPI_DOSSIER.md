# Mississippi — Wilkinson County, c.1810–1869

## Executive Summary

This dossier continues **Barnabas Partin's** biography from `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`: his relocation to Wilkinson County, Mississippi around 1810–1813, two federal land patents, a documented household across the 1820, 1830, 1840, 1850, and 1860 censuses, his death in 1855, and his wife Margaret's death in 1864. It also carries forward his son **William Partin**, who remained in Wilkinson County and administered his father's estate, and the couple's other children as far as the record permits. This is the best-documented generation in the entire project, both because it lies inside the direct scope of the externally authored Jim Partin book and because federal land patent records are independently verifiable through the General Land Office.

## Identity and Name Variants

Barnabas / Barnaby / "Barney" Partin; Margaret / "Peggy" Partin (wife).

## Geographic Footprint

Wilkinson County, Mississippi, in the vicinity of the Newman Family Cemetery, approximately 6 miles west of Centreville and 8 miles east of Woodville; Buffaloe Creek (the 1837–1852 second tract).

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented (per the Jim Partin book's register entry for Barnabas and Margaret):**
- Children: William (ca.1801–1869), Eli (ca.1803–ca.1843), Thomas (ca.1805–ca.1840), Anna (ca.1807–ca.1885), an unnamed daughter (ca.1811–ca.1825, died young), John (ca.1813–ca.1864), Sarah "Sally" (ca.1815–1877), a second unnamed daughter (ca.1818–ca.1825, died young), and Alfred W. (1824–1862).

**William Partin (son) — carried forward in Mississippi and Louisiana:**
Born ca.1801, Kershaw Co., Sumter District, SC (i.e., before the family's Mississippi relocation); died Sept 1869–1870, Wilkinson Co. MS; married Margaret "Peggy" [surname not given] in 1828 (Wilkinson Co. MS Marriage Book E, p.32); moved to Yazoo Co. MS, bought/sold land there 1830–1837 (Yazoo Co. Land Deeds Book C, pp.118, 146), then returned to Wilkinson Co.; registered a federal land patent in 1837 (75.89 acres); his wife Margaret Partain reportedly died Sept 1850 of fever, age 41, per 1850 Mortality Records; William was named administrator of his father Barnabas's estate in 1855, and after his mother Margaret's 1864 death was named guardian of nephew Alfred Partin (a minor heir of Barnabas's estate); Barnabas & Margaret's 240-acre home tract was sold at a "Guardian's Sale," 15 May 1866, to Mrs. Nancy Newman for $1,000 (recorded 14 Jun 1866, Book S p.605); William sold further tracts in 1868–1869; died in Wilkinson Co. before the 1870 census, or possibly relocated to Louisiana to live with a son, William F. Partin.

## Land and Property

Two federal patents (1819: ~162 acres; 1833: 40.76 acres); the combined 242.64-acre home tract sold 1836; the 544.23-acre Buffaloe Creek tract bought 1837 and sold 1852; a return to the original home tract, sold at guardian's sale in 1866 after both Barnabas's and Margaret's deaths. William Partin separately held land in Yazoo County, MS (1830–1837) and his own Wilkinson County patent (1837).

## Probate and Estate

Barnabas's will (Wilkinson Co. MS Probate Wills Book 2, p.100; Probate Minutes Vol. 9, pp.366–367) names enslaved individuals Tom, Matilda, and Penny — recorded here as historical fact, not endorsed. Margaret's estate was finally settled in 1868–1869, with an heir list that does not fully match the register's own child list (see Contradictions).

## Contradictions

**Birth date** (three variants, none resolved):
1. 29 Aug 1773, Halifax County, NC — the Jim Partin book's own stated date.
2. Roughly August 1772, implied by the book's own cited *Woodville Republican* obituary ("82 years, 7 months" at death).
3. Roughly 1769, implied by the book's own cited *Fort Adams News* obituary ("aged 86 years" at death) — about four years off from the book's stated date.

**Death date** (two variants):
1. 24 March 1855 — the book's stated date.
2. "Died the 25th" — per the same *Fort Adams News* obituary, a one-day conflict with the book's own stated date.

These variants are preserved deliberately rather than resolved, per this project's evidence standard. Note that the book's own cited sources (its two newspaper obituaries) disagree with the book's own headline dates — a useful reminder that even a well-cited secondary compilation can contain unreconciled internal tension.

**Margaret's 1868 estate-account heir list** includes names (Samuel, Samuel Jr., Ellen, Mary, Emily) not present in the register's own child list for Barnabas and Margaret — unreconciled.

## Evidence Assessment

- **PROVEN:** The two federal land patents (1819, 1833) and the associated Wilkinson Co. MS land transactions (1836, 1837, 1852, 1866) — federal and county records with specific citations, independently verifiable record types.
- **STRONG:** Household composition across the 1820–1860 censuses; William's Wilkinson/Yazoo land activity and role as estate administrator/guardian.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Exact birth/death dates for Barnabas; the reconciliation of Margaret's estate heir list with the earlier register.
- **CANDIDATE:** William Hux as Margaret's brother; the identity of the "Miss Parton" in the 1844 newspaper item as Barnabas's granddaughter.
