# Barnabas Partin (1773–1855) — Halifax NC → Kershaw/Sumter SC → Wilkinson Co. MS

## Executive Summary

Barnabas "Barney" Partin is the best-documented individual in this project's Carolinas-to-Mississippi generation. He is named as a son in John Partin's 1795 Halifax County will, married Margaret "Peggy" Hux around 1793, held and sold land in the Kershaw/Camden District of South Carolina between roughly 1797 and 1811, appears on the Wilkinson County, Mississippi tax rolls from 1813, obtained two federal land patents there (1819 and 1833), and died in 1855. This reconstruction is now substantially strengthened by a September 2026 recovery of the project's Google Drive corpus, which located the canonical source underlying much of the project's prior Barnabas material: a book-length compiled genealogy, *Barnabas Partin: Ancestors and Descendants, From Virginia to Mississippi and Beyond*, by Jim (Jimmy Dail) Partin — a cousin of the project owner and, per internal evidence, the same "Jim Partin" who submitted a 1997 NCGenWeb research query about this family (see `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/OLD_QUERY_PROVENANCE.md`). This dossier integrates that book's factual content, in summary and short direct quotation only, alongside the AI-assisted research previously compiled by the project.

## Identity and Name Variants

Barnabas / Barnaby / "Barney" / "Barnabus" (the last spelling appears in at least one deed-index entry recovered by the project, preserved here as an original spelling variant rather than corrected).

## Geographic Footprint

Halifax County, North Carolina (birth, 1795 will as an heir); Claremont/Kershaw County, South Carolina, Sumter District (residence and land ownership, c.1797–1811, in the immediate neighborhood of the Hux family); Wilkinson County, Mississippi (residence from c.1810/1813 until his death in 1855, land near present-day Centreville/Woodville).

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented:**
- Father: John Partin (d. 1795, Halifax Co.) — per the version of the will corroborated across the largest number of sources.
- Wife: Margaret "Peggy" [Hux?] (b. 1783 per the book, "possibly Claremont County (now Kershaw), Camden District, South Carolina"; d. 1864, Wilkinson Co. MS).
- Siblings (per the same will): Robert "Robin" (executor), Brasel, David, Charles, Lucy, Sarah, Mary, Priscilla, Elizabeth.
- Children (per the Jim Partin book's register entry): William (ca.1801–1869), Eli (ca.1803–ca.1843), Thomas (ca.1805–ca.1840), Anna (ca.1807–ca.1885), an unnamed daughter (ca.1811–ca.1825, died young), John (ca.1813–ca.1864), Sarah "Sally" (ca.1815–1877), a second unnamed daughter (ca.1818–ca.1825, died young), and Alfred W. (1824–1862).
- Margaret's estate final account (Dec 1868, closed Feb 1869) names heirs: William Partin, Mrs. R.M. "Sarah" Newman, Samuel Partin, Samuel Partin Jr., Ellen Partin, Mary Partin, Mrs. Emily Nickens, Jane Castello and husband John Castello, Sarah Partin (remarried, name not given), and Mrs. "Anna" Maxey and husband — this list includes several names (Samuel, Samuel Jr., Ellen, Mary, Emily) not present in the 1820/1830 census household or the register's child list above, and is preserved here as recovered rather than reconciled; it may include grandchildren, in-laws, or children the book's author did not separately register.

**Working hypothesis, not proven:**
- Margaret's maiden name as Hux — see `MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claim 14. The strongest single piece of evidence (the 6 Mar 1811 dower-relinquishment deed) is narrated but uncited.
- William Hux (age 63), found residing in Barnabas and Margaret's 1850 household, is speculated by the book's author to be "possibly Margaret's brother" — explicitly labeled speculation, not proven.

## Land and Property

South Carolina: witness/neighbor to Hux-family land in Kershaw Co. from 1801; sold 353 acres, Kershaw District, 1811 (uncited). Mississippi: 1819 federal patent, ~162 acres (Sec. 35, Twp. 2, Rge. 1W); 1833 federal patent, 40.76 acres (Sec. 27, Twp. 2N, Rge. 1W); sold the combined 242.64-acre home tract in 1836; bought 544.23 acres on Buffaloe Creek in 1837; sold that tract in 1852 and returned to the original home tract, where he and Margaret both died.

## Probate and Estate

Barnabas's will names enslaved individuals Tom, Matilda, and Penny — recorded here as historical fact, not endorsed. Cited to Wilkinson Co. MS Probate Wills Book 2, p. 100, and Probate Minutes Vol. 9, pp. 366–367; not independently reviewed by this project.

## Court Records

The 20 Jan 1801 Kershaw deed involving Samuel Payne and Robert White, on which Barnabas served as a witness, was sworn before "Dr. Ballard J.P." — this and related depositions are recorded in a separately compiled Surry/Kershaw-focused registry with FamilySearch ARK citations, corroborating the Jim Partin book's account independently. ## Migration

Halifax Co. NC → Kershaw/Sumter District SC (c.1797, reasoned rather than directly documented) → Wilkinson Co. MS (c.1810–1813, reasoned from four converging circumstantial points, explicitly not proven by a single migration record).

## DNA Evidence

A Y-DNA kit (MK85913), administered by the project owner and claimed as a direct paternal-line descendant through this Barnabas Partin, is reported as an exact match (0 genetic distance, 700 markers, terminal SNP R-FTF47086) to other FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project testers who have entered "Robert Partin b. 1655 England" as their own earliest known paternal ancestor. **This corroborates that these testers share a recent common paternal ancestor; it does not independently prove descent from any specific named historical individual**, since each tester's "earliest known ancestor" entry is itself an unverified genealogical claim, not a laboratory fact. ## Competing Reconstructions

None material beyond the birth/death date variants below — Barnabas is, by a wide margin, the most consistently documented individual in this generation of the project, likely because he is the direct subject of the externally authored Jim Partin book.

## Contradictions

**Birth date** — three variants preserved, none resolved:
1. 29 Aug 1773 (the register's own stated date).
2. Roughly August 1772, implied by the *Woodville Republican* obituary's stated age at death ("82 years, 7 months").
3. Roughly 1769, implied by the *Fort Adams News* obituary's stated age ("aged 86 years") — about four years off from the register's own date.

**Death date** — two variants:
1. 24 March 1855 (the register's stated date).
2. "Died the 25th," per the *Fort Adams News* obituary — a one-day conflict with the register.

These variants are preserved deliberately rather than resolved, per this project's evidence standard. Note that the register's own cited sources (its two newspaper obituaries) disagree with the register's own headline dates — a useful reminder that even a well-cited secondary compilation can contain unreconciled internal tension.

## Rejected Claims

None specific to Barnabas himself have been formally tested and rejected in the recovered corpus.

## Evidence Assessment

- **PROVEN:** Federal land patents (1819, 1833) and the associated Wilkinson Co. MS land transactions (1836, 1837, 1852) — federal and county records with specific citations, independently verifiable record types.
- **STRONG:** Halifax birth and 1795 will status as a son (pending the will-citation resolution in `JOHN_PARTIN_1762_1795_DOSSIER.md`); Kershaw Co. SC residence and land transactions 1801–1811; Wilkinson Co. MS tax and census presence from 1813.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Exact birth/death dates; wife's Hux origin; migration route and timing (VA-NC-SC-MS as a continuous, dated chain).
- **CANDIDATE:** Asa B. Partin as a son of Barnabas's brother Brasel; William Hux (in the 1850 household) as Margaret's brother.

## Open Questions

1. Locate the deed book and page for the 6 March 1811 Kershaw District, SC sale to Thomas English — the single strongest lead for proving Margaret's Hux parentage.
2. Resolve the birth/death date conflicts against the original obituary clippings and the Wilkinson Co. probate file.
3. Reconcile Margaret's 1868 estate-account heir list (which includes names — Samuel, Samuel Jr., Ellen, Mary, Emily — not present in the register's own child list) against the census and probate records.
4. Confirm or disprove Asa B. Partin's parentage from Brasel Partin, which the book's own author states "must be proven."
