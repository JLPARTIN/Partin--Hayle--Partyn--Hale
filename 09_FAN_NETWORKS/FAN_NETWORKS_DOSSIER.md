# FAN Networks — Friends, Associates, and Neighbors Across the Project

## Purpose and Method

This dossier consolidates the project's Friends-Associates-Neighbors (FAN) research across every era, since the same discipline applies throughout: for every associate, create a dated event link, not a vague "associated surname." Repeated links across jurisdictions and generations receive priority, because they can distinguish real migration from same-name coincidence. **No surname is ever treated as hereditary evidence by itself** — every FAN link in this project must be dated, sourced, and assigned a relationship type before it carries any weight in an identity argument.

## England / Jamestown Virginia, 1620s–1657

**Core Partin household (1624/25 muster):** Robert Partin Sr., Margaret Partin, children Avis, Rebecca, Robert Jr.; servants Thomas Hale and Ellin Cooke. See `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA_DOSSIER.md`.

**Shirley Hundred land-chain associates (1655 Llewellyn patent):** Daniel Llewellyn/Luellin, John Meares, Walter Aston, Edward Gardner, Edward Madison/Maddin, Sergeant John Harris, Michael Turpin, Joseph Royall, Dorothy Baker/John Baker. Search these people in deeds, patents, wills, and court minutes for Partin witnesses, adjoining tracts, prior conveyances, estate debts, and marriage/guardianship connections. A surviving deed in another family's papers may preserve a Partin relationship lost from the Partin record series itself.

**1637 lease neighbors:** Widow Thompson, Robert Brasheare/Brasseur, Fort Field, the church/glebe. The Brasseur/Brasheare family research places Robert Brasseur in the colony by 1637 and later in the Upper Norfolk/Nansemond corridor — worth following to reconstruct the geographic corridor around Robert Partin's Elizabeth City property, without inferring kinship from adjacency alone.

**1627 court proceedings:** Charles Maxey (co-defendant, ordered as Thomas Hayle's executioner).

## Surry/Sussex Virginia, 1702–1772

**Rawlings-Tucker-Justice complex:**
- **Rawlings** — strongest family-network evidence in the whole Virginia generation: John Rawlings names daughter "Eliza. Partin" by 1702/03 and conveys Blackwater land to Robert Partin in 1706.
- **Tucker** — Robert and Charles Partin both appear in Tucker estate material, 1721–22; their exact roles (debtor, creditor, witness, appraiser, purchaser) have not yet been extracted from an original.
- **Justice** — John Justice buys Robert Partin's 200-acre Blackwater tract in March 1727; determine whether this is the same tract as the 1706 Rawlings conveyance, and whether Justice reappears with Partins afterward.

**Justice-Cooper-Rawlings land network (1724–1755):** Gregory Rawlings (Elizabeth Partin's documented brother) → William Cooper (1724 grantee) → later deeds place John Justice and William Cooper as adjoining landowners, meaning Robert Partin's grantee and Gregory Rawlings's grantee occupied the same reconstructed neighborhood. Nodes: Richard Washington (common boundary anchor across 1724 and 1727), Richard Shockey (1724 boundary), John Nicholson, Benjamin Ricker (later neighborhood, 1754–55).

**Richard Washington title-chain plan:** Washington is a high-value non-Partin research target, appearing as an adjoining owner in both the 1724 Gregory Rawlings and 1727 Robert Partin transactions. Recovering his own deeds/patents across 1690–1740 — every tract on or near Blackwater, prior owner, acreage, boundary neighbors, creek/swamp/branch names, later grantee, witnesses — could use his land as a fixed edge against which the Rawlings and Partin parcels are reconstructed; a neighbor's title chain sometimes preserves a tract's history (explicitly naming a boundary as "Partin's" or "Rawlings's") more clearly than the target family's own surviving records.

**Hux (Surry seed):** William Partin and Elizabeth Hux co-occur as witnesses to a 1737 Surry will. This is the first appearance of a FAN connection — the Hux surname — that recurs in Halifax NC (1767) and in South Carolina with Barnabas Partin's own wife (1790s–1800s). See the three-generation Hux hypothesis below.

**Research principle:** a migration case becomes much stronger when independent associates migrate together, not merely when the same surname recurs. The Hux line currently offers this project's clearest test of that principle.

## The Hux-Partin Network — Three Generations

- **1737, Surry, VA** — William Partin and Elizabeth Hux, co-witnesses.
- **1767, Halifax, NC** — William and Ann Hux convey 100 acres adjoining Great Darlling Swamp to John Partin (reported; not yet image-verified).
- **1790s–1800s, Kershaw District, SC** — Barnabas Partin's wife repeatedly identified as Margaret/Peggy Hux; William Hux's own Kershaw land (from an 1786 grant) directly borders Barnabas's land.

**Required proof chain, not yet completed by this project:** identify Elizabeth Hux (1737) and reconstruct her household/parents/siblings independently; identify William and Ann Hux (Halifax, 1767) and William Hux (Kershaw, 1790s–1800s) independently; determine whether any of these Hux individuals are kin to each other; trace the Hux family's own migration independently of the Partin research that keeps surfacing them; compare witnesses and neighbors across all three deeds. This is a promising FAN hypothesis, not a proven migration conclusion — see the full discussion and contamination-risk analysis in `05_SOUTH_CAROLINA/SOUTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`.

## Halifax/Edgecombe, North Carolina, 1762–1795

**Fort-Partin network:** Elias Fort, the 1772 Edgecombe grantee from John Partin, may provide an independent associate for distinguishing the Halifax John and reconstructing his neighborhood. Recovering the full 1772 deed, mapping the land, identifying witnesses, reconstructing Elias Fort's own Virginia/NC origins, and testing whether Fort appears with Partins before Halifax remain open tasks. Do not convert repeated neighborhood contact into kinship without evidence.

**Neighborhood extraction protocol:** for every John Partin record from 1762–1795, extract all surrounding names from tax lists (adjacent taxpayers, district captain), deeds (grantors, grantees, witnesses, adjoining owners), road orders (overseer and road hands), probate (witnesses, securities, appraisers, buyers), and court minutes (co-defendants, petitioners, jurors). Build a frequency table of associates; any name appearing in three or more independent record types becomes a priority FAN candidate. Cross-state test: search high-frequency Halifax associates backward in Virginia and forward with Barnabas into South Carolina/Mississippi.

**William Reeves** — the household under whom John Partin's earliest (1762) Halifax tax entry is listed, himself reported as a Virginia migrant.
