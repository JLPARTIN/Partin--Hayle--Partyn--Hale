# Surry and Sussex Counties, Virginia — c.1700–1762

## Executive Summary

Between the 1655 Shirley Hundred land chain (see `02_JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA/JAMESTOWN_VIRGINIA_DOSSIER.md`) and this project's Halifax County, NC anchor (see `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/NORTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`) lies a Surry/Sussex County, Virginia generation documented through deeds, an estate, and scattered court/tithable appearances. A Robert Partin received 200 acres on Blackwater Swamp from John Rawlings in 1706, sold land on Blackwater to John Justice in 1727, and died around 1746, with a John Partin receiving a disputed payment from his estate. At least four other adult Partin men — Charles, William, Henry, and a second-generation Robert Jr. — appear independently in the same records and must not be silently merged into a simple father-son chain. This dossier consolidates fifty-three separate incremental research-lead files previously scattered across this directory into one narrative, preserving every genuinely open question and every internal contradiction the prior research surfaced, per this project's evidence standard.

## Identity and Name Variants

Partin / Parton / Partine / Parteyne / Pertin / Partain. This generation includes at least: Robert Partin (the primary Blackwater-tract figure, 1706–1746), Robert Partin Jr. (documented independently as a second-generation adult by 1756 and 1769), Charles Partin, William Partin, Henry Partin, and Richard Partin.

## Geographic Footprint

Southwark Parish and Blackwater Swamp, Surry County, Virginia (Robert's primary land); Albemarle Parish, spanning the Surry/Sussex border (William's and Ephraim Parton's church-warden and processioning activity); Sussex County, formed 1754 from Surry (jurisdictional transition affecting land records after that date — an open research question about which records moved to the new county).

## Family Relationships

**Explicitly documented, with direct primary-type quotation:**
- Robert Partin's wife **Elizabeth**, named in the 21 Jun 1727 dower-relinquishment acknowledgment on the Blackwater tract sold to John Justice.
- Robert Partin's wife (or a second wife) **Mary**, named in an undated ca.1746 deed to William Moore and granted Letters of Administration for his estate in late 1746.
- John Rawlings's will (1702/03) names his daughter as **"Eliza. Partin"** — this Elizabeth is a leading, though unproven, candidate to be Robert's wife (see the identity trial below).

**Strongly inferred, not proven:**
- Charles Partin as a brother or close kinsman of Robert — inferred from their joint 1721–22 Tucker-estate appearance and 1769 tithable co-listing, not from any document that states the relationship.
- "John Partin" (the 1746 estate payee) as Robert's son — see `00_PROJECT_CONTROL/MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claim 8: the payment record does not use the word "son," and this project's most conservative documents treat this link as unresolved, not proven, regardless of how confidently other recovered documents (including the project owner's own Jamestowne Society application draft) state it.

**Explicitly and correctly rejected within this project's own prior research:**
- The characterization of Charles Partin as John Rawlings III's "brother-in-law," sourced to a derivative compilation's own parenthetical hedge — "his brother in law **(I THINK)** Charles Partin" — is the *compiler's own proposed identification*, not quoted kinship language from any deed. This must not be cited as though the original 1706 deed itself calls Charles a brother-in-law.
- A generic online pedigree numbering Robert I (immigrant) → Robert II (1624–1687) → Robert III (1655–1727) → Robert IV (1680–1743) → John (1722–1795), with "Mary Alice Reviea" attached to Robert II and "Elizabeth Rawlings" attached to Robert III, was specifically audited by this project. **No primary documentation was located supporting the precise birth/death dates or this numbered Robert III/IV parentage sequence.** These remain UNPROVEN TREE DATA and should not be repeated as fact merely because they are widely circulated online.

## Primary and Near-Primary Evidence — The Elizabeth Rawlings Identity Trial

This is the single most developed proof problem in the Surry/Sussex generation, and it remains genuinely unresolved. The starting fact is secure: John Rawlings's 1702/03 will names daughter **"Eliza. Partin"** — she was already married to a Partin man by August 1702. Her husband's given name is never stated in the will itself.

**Model A — Robert Partin, the Blackwater-tract man.** Positive evidence: the 1706 Rawlings-to-Robert-Partin Blackwater deed; Robert's 1727 sale of the same class of land to John Justice; the 21 Jun 1727 Elizabeth dower-relinquishment acknowledgment tied to that sale; a shared Richard Washington boundary between Robert's 1727 tract and a 1724 Gregory Rawlings (Elizabeth's documented brother) conveyance, placing Robert inside the reconstructed Rawlings family land neighborhood. Missing: an original acknowledgment that explicitly and unambiguously identifies "Elizabeth" as "Elizabeth Rawlings"; independent proof that the 1706 and 1727 parcels are the identical tract rather than merely similar-sized Blackwater land.

**Model B — Charles Partin.** Positive evidence: a derivative Rawlings-family compilation's repeated (but never book/page-cited) assertion that Charles received Rawlings-connected land in 1706 and sold land "late in the tenure of John Rawlings" in 1727. Missing: the actual 1706 deed (its existence is currently **downgraded to unverified**, not merely unconfirmed — see Contradictions); the actual 1727 deed; any wife/dower acknowledgment naming Charles's wife.

**Current judgment, preserved as the prior research left it:** neither husband is proved. Robert currently has the more developed, independently multi-sourced document chain (1706 deed with a specific citation; two 1727 instruments with verbatim quoted language; the Richard Washington/Gregory Rawlings land-network corroboration). Charles remains a legitimate competing hypothesis specifically because of the repeated Rawlings-land association reported for his 1727 transaction — but that association currently rests on a single derivative compilation that itself confuses Robert and Charles within the same narrative and supplies no primary citation for the Charles-specific deeds. **This dossier does not resolve the identity trial; it preserves it exactly as an open question requiring the original 1706 and 1727 Charles-attributed instruments.**

### A note on Elizabeth Rawlings identity collisions

At least two distinct "Elizabeth Rawlings" women are attested in Surry-era records and must not be conflated:
- **Elizabeth A** — daughter of John Rawlings (testator 1702/03), siblings John and Gregory, called "Eliza. Partin" by Aug 1702 — the subject of this identity trial.
- **Elizabeth B** — daughter of a separate Roger and Alice (Newsom) Rawlings household, who married William Pettaway/Petway and later John Davis, per an independently published Virginia genealogical article, with her own documentary trail through Petway estate records and a 1705 deed.

Records of Elizabeth Petway/Davis must never be used to fill gaps in Elizabeth Partin's biography. Same surname, same county, and same generation is not identity.

A separate 1697 Surry will abstract (Anna Jordan, Will Book 5 p.147) bequeaths clothing to "Elizabeth Rawlings" and is speculated by one compiler to be the same Elizabeth before her marriage — narrowing a possible marriage window to 1697–Aug 1702 if confirmed — but the abstract's date sequence is internally confusing ("2 Oct., 1697. 22 July, 1697") by the compiler's own admission, and the relationship between the Anna Jordan household and John Rawlings's family (hypothesized as both belonging to a reconstructed Mary Sowerby-connected household) is not established. This remains a CANDIDATE lead, not a confirmed pre-marriage sighting.

## The Blackwater Land-Parcel Network

The next real breakthrough on the Elizabeth Rawlings question is expected to come from land-identity proof, not name-similarity reasoning. The fixed anchors:

- **1706** — John Rawlings → Robert Partin, 200 acres, north side Blackwater Swamp.
- **1724** — Gregory & Hannah Rawlings → William Cooper, 489 acres, adjoining **Richard Washington** and Richard Shockey (Surry Deeds/Wills Etc. Book 7 Part 2, pp.588–589).
- **1727** — Robert Partin → John Justice, 200 acres on Blackwater Swamp, adjoining **Richard Washington** (Book 7 Part 3, pp.726–727).
- **1754** — John and Henry Person → Richard Wiggins Jr., 213 acres adjoining William Cooper, John Justice, John Nicholson, and Benjamin Ricker (Surry Deed Book 7, 1753–1760, pp.128–130).
- **1755** — Richard and Tabitha Wiggins → Michael Carsley, same 213-acre neighborhood, adjoining William Cooper, John Justice, Benjamin Rickes, and John Nicholson (pp.187–188).

The 1727 Robert Partin tract shares the Richard Washington boundary with Gregory Rawlings's 1724 tract, and Robert's grantee John Justice later appears as an adjoining owner to William Cooper (Gregory Rawlings's own grantee) in 1754–1755. **This is independent geographic evidence tying Robert's tract into a reconstructable Rawlings family land neighborhood** — it strengthens, but does not by itself prove, that Robert's Blackwater tract was part of the Rawlings family land complex, and by extension that Robert married into that family. What would close this chain: matching enough metes-and-bounds language between the 1706 and 1727 deeds to establish they concern the identical 200-acre tract, and an original acknowledgment explicitly naming Elizabeth's origin.

**Richard Washington** himself is a high-value non-Partin research target: recovering his own deeds/patents (search window ca.1690–1740) may preserve earlier owners and resolve which Rawlings parcel passed into Partin hands, since neighboring deeds sometimes preserve a tract's history more clearly than the target family's own surviving records.

## Charles Partin

Known/reported anchors: joint appearance with Robert in the 1721–22 Tucker estate material; the disputed/unverified 1706 and 1727 Rawlings-connected land associations discussed above; the ca.1748 Benjamin Emmery debt suit as defendant; joint listing with Robert Jr. on the 1769 Southwark tithables ("Robt Partin and Charles Partin — 2"). Charles's relationship to Robert (brother, cousin, or otherwise) is not established by any document recovered — it is inferred solely from repeated co-occurrence. He remains important because his disputed Rawlings-land association complicates any simple assignment of "Eliza. Partin" to Robert alone, and any future research must recover his own deeds, court orders, and tithable entries independently before assigning him a parentage or relationship.

## William Partin

Independently documented as an adult Surry/Albemarle Parish resident across at least three decades: 1737 (will-witness alongside Elizabeth Hux), 1747/48–1763 (land processioning, Albemarle Parish Vestry Book), 1753 (witness, Rochell-to-Hearn deed, Surry Deed Book 7 p.47), and 1771–72 (parish poor-relief payments for "keeping Rebeckah"). William's durability across so many independent record types means the Surry Partin family structure cannot safely be reduced to a chain of men all named Robert. His parentage, land ownership, and any migration are not established; he should continue to be reconstructed independently before being fit into any pedigree, and his connection (if any) to Elizabeth Hux beyond the single 1737 witness event remains untested — see the Hux FAN-network discussion below.

## Henry Partin

Documented only as an adult defendant in a discontinued petition case, Surry Co., ca.1753–1757 ("Idam — Henry Partin — Deft — By Petition — Discontinued the Plaintif failing to prosecute"). Any adult Henry in this period must be integrated into the family reconstruction rather than omitted for not fitting a familiar numbered-Robert pedigree. His relationship to Robert's 1746 estate, land, tithables, wife, and children are all unrecovered.

## The Virginia Side of the John Partin / Halifax Bridge Problem

A "John Partin" appears twice in this Virginia generation's record set: as the disputed 1746 estate payee from Robert's estate (above), and — per some recovered documents — as a Sussex Co. tithable in 1761 who is then absent from the 1763 Sussex tithable list, roughly contemporary with a John Partin's first appearance on the 1762 Halifax Co., NC tax list. **Whether the Surry/Sussex John Partin and the Halifax John Partin are the same man is this project's single most important unresolved identity bridge, and it is covered in full in `04_HALIFAX_NORTH_CAROLINA/NORTH_CAROLINA_DOSSIER.md`, not here** — this Surry/Sussex dossier's role is to establish clearly that no primary document recovered by this project states a Virginia residence for the Halifax John, or a North Carolina destination for the Virginia John. A commonly discussed alternative identification — a "John Charles" baptism dated 16 Mar 1733 — has also been proposed for this slot in some derivative research; neither the traditional early-1720s birth estimate nor the 1733 candidate should control the identification until primary records establish ages and relationships.

## Rawlings Family Structure — Anti-Conflation Reference

Two distinct John Rawlings households must be kept separate:
- **John Rawlings, d.1673/74** — will names daughter Elizabeth Rawlings, wife Eleanor, minor children Mary and John.
- **John Rawlings, d.1702/03** — per derivative reconstruction, son of the preceding household; will names son John (Blackwater land), son Gregory (home plantation, 126 acres), daughter Eliza. Partin, wife Mary (executrix).

The 1702/03 will's sibling group — John, Gregory, and Eliza. Partin — is the operative one for this dossier's Elizabeth Rawlings identity trial. Gregory's identity as Elizabeth's brother (and therefore the 1724 Gregory & Hannah Rawlings deed as her brother's land activity) depends on confirming that the 1724 Gregory is the same man named in the 1702/03 will — chronology and land context favor this, but it has not been independently demonstrated by this project.

## Contradictions

- **Elizabeth (1727) vs. Mary (1746)** as Robert Partin's wife — two wives in succession, or a naming/transcription variance, remains unreconciled. The compiling source itself flags this rather than resolving it.
- **Mary granted Letters of Administration (late 1746) vs. Robert Connell shown as the actual estate-account administrator (20 Aug 1746)** — unreconciled by any recovered document.
- **The 1746 estate payment to John Partin: "592" vs. "3 Pounds, 15 Shillings"** — two figures for what multiple documents describe as the same transaction; see `10_SOURCES/SOURCE_CONTRADICTIONS/MASTER_CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md` #5.
- **The disputed second 1706 Rawlings-to-Charles-Partin deed** — asserted repeatedly in one derivative compilation, but that same compilation internally substitutes Robert's name when describing what should be the Charles transaction, and supplies no book/page citation anywhere. Status downgraded to unverified.
- A FamilySearch automated transcript misread "Richard Partin and Son" as "Richard Cocke and Son" in the 1753–1757 Order Book — corrected here from the index reference and context, and preserved as a standing caution about trusting automated transcription in this record series generally.

## Rejected Claims

- A specific separate 1706 John Rawlings → Charles Partin deed "existing" as a known record — this was an assumption carried forward from an internally inconsistent derivative webpage and should not be repeated as established; search for such a deed as a falsification test, not as retrieval of a presumed-known record.
- Charles Partin as John Rawlings III's documented "brother-in-law" — the source phrase itself ("I THINK") marks this as the compiler's inference, not deed language.
- The generic online "Robert I → Robert II (1624–1687) → Robert III (1655–1727) → Robert IV (1680–1743) → John (1722–1795)" numbered pedigree, with attached wives "Mary Alice Reviea" and "Elizabeth Rawlings" — audited and found unsupported by primary documentation for its precise dates and generational assignments.

## Evidence Assessment

- **STRONG:** Robert Partin's 1706 Rawlings land acquisition; his 1727 sale to John Justice with Elizabeth's dower release; his estate's existence and administration dispute in 1743/44–1746; John Rawlings's 1702/03 will naming "Eliza. Partin"; William Partin's multi-decade documented presence; Henry and Richard Partin as newly attested independent adults; the Robert Jr. second-generation identification via the 1756 and 1769 records.
- **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** Elizabeth ("Eliza. Partin") as Robert's (rather than Charles's) wife; the Richard Washington/Gregory Rawlings land-network corroboration as proof of the Rawlings-Partin family land complex; "John Partin" (1746 payee) as Robert's son.
- **CANDIDATE:** The 1697 Anna Jordan pre-marriage Elizabeth Rawlings sighting; Charles as Robert's brother; a 1706 Rawlings-to-Charles land conveyance.
- **REJECTED:** The numbered "Robert I–IV" online pedigree with its attached dates and wives; Charles as documented "brother-in-law" to John Rawlings III; a confirmed separate 1706 Charles deed.
