# Rawlings–Partin Breakthrough — 1702/03 to 1727

## Critical new relationship evidence
A compiled transcription of the **will of John Rawlings**, made August 1702 and proved 25 May 1703, states:
**“Daughter, Eliza. Partin”** was to receive livestock and other property already in her possession.

This is a major clue. It demonstrates that an **Elizabeth Partin was a daughter of John Rawlings** before the 1706 Rawlings→Partin land transaction.

## 1706 land transaction
Surry Deeds No. 5, p. 348 is transcribed:
John Rawlings of Southwark Parish → Robert Partin of same, **200 acres north side Blackwater Swamp**, land taken up by the grantor's father Jonathan/John Rawlings by patent 29 Apr 1692; recorded 7 May 1706.

## 1727 follow-through
The same Rawlings compilation reports that Charles Partin later sold land described as **“late in the tenure of John Rawlings.”**

## What this changes
The Rawlings–Partin connection is not simply neighboring landowners. A Rawlings daughter was already married into the Partin surname by 1702.

This substantially strengthens the hypothesis that the 1706 conveyance occurred inside a family/FAN relationship.

## What remains unresolved
The will excerpt says **Eliza. Partin**, but does not identify her husband in the quoted text. It therefore does NOT yet prove:
- Elizabeth was wife of Robert Partin;
- Elizabeth was wife of Charles Partin;
- which Partin generation she married.

Those possibilities must be tested against deeds, dower releases, wills and chronology.
