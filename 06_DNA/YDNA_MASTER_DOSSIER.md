# Y-DNA Master Dossier

## Executive Summary

This project has two Y-DNA data sources recovered from the project owner's Google Drive (September 2026): a raw 19-row Y-STR/haplogroup spreadsheet apparently used to visually cluster FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project participants, and a separate narrative claim — recorded in a draft Jamestowne Society application — that the project owner's own kit (MK85913) is an exact 700-marker match to other testers claiming descent from "Robert Partin b. 1655 England," under terminal SNP R-FTF47086. **These two sources cannot currently be cross-referenced: SNP R-FTF47086 and kit MK85913 do not appear anywhere in the 19-row spreadsheet.** This dossier keeps genetic fact and genealogical claim strictly separate throughout, per `MASTER_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` Claim 16 and the project's founding evidence standard: DNA is corroborative evidence, not a substitute for documentary parentage.

**Reading this data:**
- All observed terminal SNPs (BY31280, FTB85652, FTE30394, M269, F22409, Y32699) are downstream subclades of **R-M269**, the common West European R1b founder SNP — consistent with, but not proof of, a single relatively recent common paternal ancestor for the group.
- **R-FTE30394** is the dominant branch in this dataset (8 of 19 rows), spanning Sevier, Cocke, and Franklin Counties, TN, and Haywood County, NC — this is very likely this project's "core" documented East Tennessee/western North Carolina Y-DNA cluster.
- **R-Y32699** (Anderson Co., SC, 2 kits) is both geographically and genetically the most distinct cluster in the dataset from the Tennessee group.
- No participant-entered "earliest known ancestor" is recorded anywhere in this sheet. **Nothing in this dataset should be read as proof of descent from any specific named colonial ancestor** — it is raw haplogroup/STR clustering data only, and the county-of-origin column records where a living participant's family is *from*, not where a specific ancestor lived at a specific date.

## The MK85913 / R-FTF47086 claim

A separate, narrative (non-spreadsheet) document recovered from the same Drive — a draft Jamestowne Society supplemental-application argument authored by the project owner — reports:

- Kit MK85913, tested to the Big Y-700 level, terminal SNP **R-FTF47086**.
- An exact match (0 genetic distance across all 700 markers tested) to other FamilyTreeDNA Partin Surname Project participants who have entered "Robert Partin, b. 1655, England" as their own earliest known paternal-line ancestor.
- Supporting autosomal (AncestryDNA) evidence for the same kit: 25–80 cM matches to descendants of John Partin (d. 1795) via his sons Charles and Priscilla's lines; 15–45 cM matches to the Surry/Sussex Virginia Partin descendant cluster; 10–35 cM matches to Hux-surnamed testers from the Kershaw, SC, 1780s–1800s cluster, offered as support for Margaret "Peggy" Hux's identity (see `05_CAROLINAS_TO_MISSISSIPPI/BARNABAS_PARTIN_DOSSIER.md`).

**What this establishes:** kits sharing a 0-genetic-distance 700-marker Y-DNA match share a genuinely recent common paternal ancestor. That is a real, meaningful genetic finding.

**What this does NOT establish:** that the shared ancestor is specifically the historical individual "Robert Partin, b. 1655, England," or any other specific named person. Each matching tester's "earliest known ancestor" field is a **documentary claim entered by that tester**, not a laboratory finding — it carries exactly the evidentiary weight of that tester's own paper-trail research, which this project has not independently reviewed for any tester besides its own line. A cluster of testers all naming the same documentary ancestor increases confidence that they researched consistently, not that the identification is correct; a shared, uncritically repeated tree error would produce the identical pattern.

**The cross-reference gap:** SNP R-FTF47086 and kit MK85913 do not appear among the 19 rows of the raw project spreadsheet above (which tops out, in this recovered extract, at R-FTE30394, R-BY31280, R-FTB85652, R-F22409, R-M269, and R-Y32699). Until this is resolved — by re-pulling MK85913's placement directly from FamilyTreeDNA's current Partin Surname Project tree — this project cannot state how MK85913 relates to the documented TN/NC/SC cluster above, or whether R-FTF47086 is a downstream refinement of one of those branches or a separate, more distantly related line entirely.

## Working branch hypothesis (carried forward from prior project sessions)

Prior research referenced a Parton/Partin/Partain FTDNA cluster restructuring beneath nodes FTT183/FTT184/FTT185, with Halifax and Sussex documentary lines remaining genetically separate from a newly merged branch. If several documented branches merge beneath a downstream SNP while the Halifax and Sussex documentary lines remain outside it, this could indicate that those two lines represent earlier-diverging paternal branches — a pattern potentially useful for locating a documentary/genetic split near the older Virginia generations. **This remains a hypothesis.** The documentary tree (built from deeds, wills, and censuses) and the genetic tree (built from SNP/STR results) should continue to be developed independently before being correlated, so that neither is used to silently validate the other.

## Rule

Y-DNA can establish or strongly support patrilineal relatedness and relative branch order among living testers. It cannot, by itself, name the historical man in whom a given mutation arose, and it cannot substitute for documentary proof of any specific parent-child link in this project's paper genealogy. Every claim in this dossier that pairs a SNP/kit with a named historical ancestor is labeled a genealogical claim, not a genetic fact, per this rule.
